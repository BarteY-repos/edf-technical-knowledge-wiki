Kill merit and Air Raider credits across three generations: score inputs and the 1500-point compression

ConversionGame technical referencesGameplay economyEDF 4.1EDF 5EDF 6
Technical reference · Source-scoped static analysis; no R129 gameplay test

Some support equipment and vehicles recharge through kill merit rather than elapsed time. The number displayed as an enemy's base HP or its level-adjusted durability is not automatically the number of credits earned. The mechanics also changed between EDF4.1 and the later games.

Which object contributes merit?

Generation Native rule established by the cited build Notes
EDF4.1 Raw game_object_durability No game_object_destroy_score_adjust key is read by the examined executable
EDF5 Raw durability × game_object_destroy_score_adjust The adjustment defaults to 1; level adjustment is not part of merit
EDF6 Raw durability × game_object_destroy_score_adjust Same model as EDF5 with different object-field offsets

At an enemy's death, the engine credits the relevant hostile teams through the TeamManager. An object without a team (-1) does not credit a team through this path. This is why the merit rule must not be interpreted as a blanket "every destroyed object gives every player the same credit" statement.

For EDF5, source examples are Silver Spider: 450 × 5 = 2250, Gold Ant: 30 × 5 = 150, and Colonist: 500 × 0.84 = 420. The separate game_object_level_adjust can scale HP but is not multiplied into the merit reward.

The original EDF4.1 Japanese merit table mostly agrees with object durability, with a few exceptions. Retiarius webs are described as granting zero in the community source, but the reason that their destruction does not pass through the usual team-credit route remains unverified in the executable. Do not infer it from the presence of an SGO durability field.

Why a huge kill does not give its full cost immediately

In EDF5 and EDF6, credit-charged equipment (ReloadType = 2) compares the TeamManager's accumulated merit against the total last observed by the weapon. For the new increment d, the observed conversion is:

[ C(d) = \begin{cases} d, & d \leq 1500 \ 1500 + \operatorname{int}((d-1500)^{0.8}), & d > 1500. \end{cases} ]

The code performs its integer conversion as part of the update. It then reduces the weapon's outstanding reload cost using C(d) and stores the latest team total. Compression happens to the accumulated increment between two weapon updates, so a large batch of kills can be compressed together.

Source example (EDF5) Raw merit credited Reload credit gained
One Silver Spider 2250 1699
One Erginus 32000 5367
100 Black Ants killed together 6000 2336

The EDF6 source has the same 1500 threshold and exponent 0.8 at a different routine address. Its TeamManager total is maintained locally for the current machine's game state, so identical credit reload behavior on every online peer cannot be inferred solely from the equation.

What remains uncertain

The EDF4.1 damage/death routine supports durability-only team merit, but the original EDF4.1 call-in reload was not fully reversed in the cited study. The EDF5/EDF6 compression rule must not be retroactively asserted as EDF4.1 behavior. Also, source examples come from specific PC builds; field layouts are not portable offsets.
