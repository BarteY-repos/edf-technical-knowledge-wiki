Why an apparently valid switch can run the wrong branch

Some EDF5-compiled switches serialize the case bodies before their comparison/dispatch ladder. A converter that assumes the dispatch precedes the case bodies can emit those physical bodies in sequence. The subsequent comparison ladder may then reduce to useless constant branches such as if (0 == 3); the last emitted case wins on every difficulty. Syntax validation and successful mission load do not catch this class of semantic error.

Three observed backwards-layout forms must be recognized:

  1. The earlier strict backward-layout case that the converter already understood.
  2. An empty-default final dispatch ending in pop, then falling directly through to the merge label.
  3. An empty-default final dispatch ending in pop; jmp MERGE.

Implementation rule: recognize these precise control-flow forms, construct the semantic switch from the source dispatch graph, preserve merge boundaries and operand typing, and fail closed on other non-canonical patterns. Do not flatten physical bytecode order into AngelScript statement order.

What was verified and what was not

The R8 source-only closure reports 141/141 regenerated source BVMs, zero remaining signatures for the missed constant-comparison shape, and 10/10 switch-patched AC validators PASS. The broader R8 Core reported 17/17 validator PASS, including independent scalar/Desert508 work. The batch was 140 clean plus DM006_2 with its unchanged six cuscall0 3450 diagnostic issues; it was byte-identical before and after the switch repair.

These are offline/structural results. R8's suggested runtime checks include M034_5 at Easy/Normal, M065 across levels, M059 at Normal/Hard and M081 offline Hard/Inferno. The later 1.0.6 BVM parity audit fixes additional mission behaviors; do not interpret R8 as replacing the later shipping/release authority. See 1.0.6 BVM parity repairs and control-flow/dataflow translation.
