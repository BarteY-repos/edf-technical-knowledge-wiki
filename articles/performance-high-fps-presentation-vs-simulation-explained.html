EDF6 high frame rates: unique visual states versus Presents

EDF 6Performance & 120 FPSPerformance
Technical reference · Mixed runtime and offline research

In EDF6, a displayed frame, a character pose, a camera state and one authoritative gameplay update are different events. Raising one frequency does not automatically raise the others.

What measurements can tell us

An original EDF6 moving-camera experiment recorded approximately 90.306 unique camera states per second, 89.728 Presents per second and a 59.922 Hz fixed cadence. The result shows that a camera can update more often than gameplay without requiring enemy AI or physics to advance at 90 Hz. It does not establish a complete 90 Hz presentation of every game subsystem.

A useful measure of higher-rate visual presentation is the number of new world/camera states per Present, not just an FPS counter. If a hypothetical 120-Hz display stream includes mostly repeated animation poses, its raw Present number overstates the actual visible motion cadence.

Native subsystems have different clocks

Character animation and skinning, vegetation, shader time, particle effects, ribbons/trails, camera transforms, visibility and collision do not necessarily share one update path. Fixed-step network/simulation code may intentionally advance at about 60 Hz even if the renderer can run faster. A game can appear smoother in one domain but remain coarse in another.

A visibility throttling experiment produced black-surface flicker and delayed shadows, illustrating why fewer occlusion passes are not automatically free performance. Likewise, asynchronously scheduled rendering must use state from the correct owned job, rather than assuming a global tick describes every concurrent task.

Confidence and limits

These are local measurements and static findings from selected PC builds and research runs. They should not be generalized into promises about all PC configurations, nor described as proof that EDF6 natively offers a fully smooth 120-Hz mode with unchanged gameplay. No new game-runtime test was conducted for this article.

For the original numeric findings see fixed cadence and Umbra visibility.
