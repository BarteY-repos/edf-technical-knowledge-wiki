EDF5 weapon and armor loot: the end-of-mission roll and class/level eligibility

EDF 5Game technical referencesPickups and rewards
Technical reference · Source-declared grades; this edition does not add native game runtime testing

Picking up a green or red item box is not the same operation as receiving a particular weapon or armor increase. In the investigated EDF5 executable, DropItemManager::Unit accounts for crates during play, while the native 1991D0 result routine later allocates weapon and armor rewards into save records. This makes the result screen a second stage of the loot system.

When boxes become rewards

Weapon and armor pickups increment distinct mission counts (DropItemManager+0xA88 and +0xA8C). The result entry collects them, combines retained counts where applicable, seeds an independent Mersenne Twister and assigns the reward categories. Weapon entries carry eight star bytes; persistence merges those into saved weapon progression by keeping each statistic's maximum grade rather than replacing a higher existing grade with a lower one.

Spawn and reward counts are subject to separate limits. The result routine cannot roll more boxes than were actually spawned in the current mission: the manager records weapon and armor spawn totals at +0xAD8 and +0xADC. The original 1024 pickup cap does not mean 1024 distinct weapons are guaranteed or available.

Which class receives a box?

The source's four-class weighted allocation starts each class at 100 for both weapon and armor. The locally played class is raised to 600 for weapon crates and 250 for armor crates. With exactly one local player:

Reward type Played class Each of the three other classes
Weapon 600/900 = 66.67% 100/900 = 11.11%
Armor 250/550 = 45.45% 100/550 = 18.18%

These probabilities are for class assignment of an eligible box and change with additional local players and game state. They are not final probabilities for a particular weapon or star grade.

Level range and candidate eligibility

A mission's position within the selected list gives a normalized progression fraction t = missionIndex / (missionCount - 1). EDF5 reads a low/high/spread triple from defaultpackage/config.sgo's ModeList[mode][6][difficulty][2]. The source-derived model uses:

  • center = low + (high - low) × t
  • width = max(0.05, 0.05 × (high - low))
  • lower edge roughly max(0, center - spread); upper edge includes a narrow positive allowance for star-bearing weapons

The routine also checks class category, acquisition status, source MissionPack (ModeList[mode][8]) and weapon-table row eligibility. Ordinary weapons may appear across packs, whereas pack-owned weapon rows are constrained to their intended pack. A JP wiki comparison found exact predicted lowest/highest levels in 473 of 550 sampled cells and predictions within one level in the other 77; this is supporting correlation, not independent binary proof of every edge case.

Failure-state retention is different

A separate EDF5 Mission Failed routine calculates a fraction of collected crates that may be retained: Easy 100%, Normal 75%, Hard 50%, Hardest 25%, Inferno 0%, truncating the number of crates. This is a result-screen count path, not a change to crate spawning itself. The source audit did not fully trace the later handoff from this screen to final rolling, so do not infer further award semantics from this fraction alone.
