EDF4.1 → EDF6 bank collisions, CRI loading and shutdown

The source-backed audio-bank and cue-resolution guide explains why 1,087 of 1,469 source SE cue names collide with native EDF6 names, why ACB and AWB can take different physical file-opening paths, and how an otherwise functioning imported sound bank can crash when released after the audio system is destroyed. Timed MAB cues and channel ownership are a separate layer; correct cue lookup alone does not preserve event timing.

Audio in EDF is not one interchangeable archive or one string table. Voice/audio banks, cue names, localized text, mission-level playback commands and subtitle rendering are separate parts of the pipeline. Our research covers pieces of that system; it does not establish a complete EDF5→EDF6 audio-format conversion.

AWE, AWB and ACB

The retained CRIWARE investigation supports inspection and extraction of the studied AWE/AWB data, including AWBE identity handling and endian-tolerant parsing in the observed tooling. These findings are enough to inspect voice-name/list data and pair metadata with bank contents in the supported research path.

They do not by themselves prove:

  • that a bank can be copied unchanged between games;
  • that cue indexes and language routing remain identical;
  • that an unknown ACB revision can be rebuilt losslessly;
  • that codec, bank or sequence playback semantics match EDF5 and EDF6.

EFARC is tracked separately as an EDF6 file/effect-archive research target. The retained work does not establish a universal EFARC parser or a rule equating it with RAB/MARC/CRIWARE audio containers.

Mission dialogue is not just a filename

The mission-scripting corpus distinguishes calls and data for voice, dialogue, background music and sound effects. A correct mission translation must preserve the relationship between:

  1. the script event that requests playback;
  2. the voice/cue/text reference it uses;
  3. the language-specific text/metadata;
  4. the assets actually included in the deployment;
  5. any event/thread sequencing affecting presentation.

A structurally valid script can still play the wrong speech, omit text or wait for a voice handshake on an event path that should not use it. The recovered event model treats SYNC and ASYNC wrappers differently for relevant voice-thread handling.

Localization merges must be conservative

A mission/localization merge must not erase unknown keys or a language variant simply because a source text table did not use it. The retained localization work treats key identity and per-language ownership as separate from the current language visible on screen.

Do not infer equivalence from English text alone. A shared identifier may refer to different localized content or gameplay ownership in another resource.

Subtitle display is another layer

The EDF6 subtitle-scroll investigation concerns the presentation/lifetime of subtitles, not a universal change to bank or codec formats. Fixing whether long captions scroll or stay visible does not prove that missing dialogue records, wrong cue references or packaging dependencies have been repaired.

What to do with an unfamiliar audio resource

Identify its container/metadata role before writing a converter. Preserve original cue and language references, inspect whether it is mission-local or game-global, and mark cross-version playback claims as pending unless the exact candidate has been tested in-game.
