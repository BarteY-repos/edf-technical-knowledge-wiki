DM001 CAVE501 / E506 — R33P-C practical compatibility closure

UniversalMissions & ScriptingMissionsEDF 5EDF 6
Source-backed research / case study · Runtime-proven / practical authority

Scope: EDF5 DLC1 MissionPack04/DM001, stock EDF6 ig_Cave501, stock EDF6 E506 Humanoid_Basic, and imported EDF5 encounter/spawn/route semantics. Current status: PRACTICALLY_ACCEPTED / NO ACTIVE MISSION BLOCKER.

This case is important because the final result is not “converted E506 is broken” and not “rewrite the navmesh.” The problematic combination was legacy EDF5 encounter placement/route semantics executed by stock EDF6 Humanoid/navigation on stock EDF6 CAVE501.

Original K failure

Original/source-like 宇宙人02 spawn briefly appeared at its intended position, entered a T-pose/activation state and was immediately relocated upward above the tunnel. The target collision reconstruction at K had approximately 15.77 units of floor-to-ceiling clearance: too small for the initial standing D10 outer height, although LOW/STOOP D18 could fit.

Extensive research established useful mechanisms, but several attractive explanations were later rejected or demoted. In particular, delaying SetAiRoute proved that the original K eject happened before route assignment, so route assignment was not the first-frame ejection trigger. Single-variable humanoid_climb_setting restoration was also runtime-negative as a standalone fix.

Geometry classifier evidence — mechanism, not universal law

The R31 audit compared K against passing O/N positions and a working native EDF6 control. The strongest hazard family involved deep standing-capsule overlap plus local roof-feature topology/edge conditions, not merely low clearance or a nearly vertical roof. No universal numeric threshold was frozen.

This evidence is valuable for validators and diagnostics, but the production converter did not ship a global engine hook or a magic geometry threshold.

Frozen practical spawn — R33O

Accepted practical spawn:

p1 = (-276.11517333984375, -73.23749542236328, -386.4794921875)
p2 = (-251.05178833007812, -43.85660934448242, -423.6900939941406)

R33O made spawn initialization usable. Its synthetic floor route prefix still took actors upward and stalled. From this point the spawn was frozen; the remaining problem was route/path branch selection.

R33P-A and R33P-B — why they failed

R33P-A: frozen spawn -> original WP0 -> original WP1..WP6. Result: upward detour still occurred.

R33P-B: original WP0 -> native NAVMESH_H face536 guide -> original WP1..WP6. Result: still upward. The key discovery was ordering: while WP0 remained the current first target, the later face536 guide could not influence initial route acquisition.

This is a general route-authoring lesson: inserting a corrective node after the node at which the wrong branch is selected cannot fix the initial branch decision.

R33P-C — route solution

R33P-C promotes the native-H lower-branch guide to the first active route target, skips original WP0 as the initial target, then preserves original WP1..WP6.

frozen R33O spawn
 -> native NAVMESH_H lower-branch guide (face536-derived)
 -> original WP1
 -> WP2 -> WP3 -> WP4 -> WP5 -> WP6

Minimal diagnostic runtime: PASS desired lower-route traversal.

Full R50 runtime: the problematic actor followed the corrected route. An initial theory that the first and second aliens had different aggro behavior was re-tested and retracted; the remaining observed problem was physical interference with SilverSpiders in the same wave, not route failure.

Final integration delta

Parity analysis showed that the earlier gray02A, not gray02B, is the actor co-waved with the SilverSpiders. The final parity-checked integration therefore delays only the earlier gray02A by 10 seconds while retaining the working R33P-C route and source timing elsewhere.

Related technical evidence: why the collision behavior differed

The separate EDF5/EDF6 humanoid capsule-contact and low-stance reference documents the matching nominal standing/low shapes, old hkp contact unwelding versus EDF6 hknp neighbor welding, and the exact-binary low/stoop selection gates. These are useful explanations of the earlier diagnostic symptoms, but they do not supersede the accepted R33P-C route or establish a global collision-engine fix.

Navigation facts retained

  • Stock E506 uses navigator layer 0 in this audited case.
  • For CAVE501, layer 0 maps to NAVMESH_H.HKT; layer 1 maps to primary NAVMESH.HKT.
  • ai_character_radius sweeps (5.00 / 4.75 / 4.65 / 3.00) did not fix the route issue.
  • m_way_point_update_distasnce extrema (8.0 / 0.5) did not fix it.
  • Blind waypoint density and world-space floor interpolation were not sufficient.
  • The decisive route correction was first-target/branch selection on the native H navigation layer.

Production constraints

The accepted solution remains content-side. It does not require an EDF.dll patch, custom runtime DLL, global native E506 mutation or global CAVE501/navmesh mutation. Native EDF6/DLC content must remain outside any generic EDF5 conversion rewrite unless separately justified.

Still open generically

DM001 itself is practically closed. What remains open is a generic source-only rule that can identify/select equivalent lower-branch guide semantics from source data across arbitrary missions/maps. R33P-C is a mission-local proven compatibility layer, not yet a universal MISSION.RMPA conversion law.

Do not repeat

Do not restart pre-R33P-C placement sweeps, delayed-route-as-initial-ejection theory, CreateEnemy2 initialization experiments, radius/distance sweeps, the retracted first-vs-second aggro theory, or treat R33P-A/B as final state.

Negative diagnostic controls and the source/runtime boundary

The initial tunnel/ejection problem was subjected to several independent negative controls, not simply fixed by reducing a humanoid radius. A native-K CreateEnemy2 candidate remained FAIL / no change at the first-frame upward-ejection point; source/binary comparison indicated it changed spawn transform-orientation construction but converged on the same Humanoid_Basic/body/controller creation. Dense local route-steering in the native tunnel remained a same-location, same-stall failure. Tested AI capsule radii 5.00 / 4.75 / 4.65 / 3.00 all failed as standalone repairs. A candidate position eventually passed the initial stoop threshold but still routed upward and stalled; only later spawn-and-first-target corrections solved the practical route.

Crucially, an apparent combat/aggro difference in the full-mission integration was explicitly re-tested and withdrawn when fire duration was controlled. That observation must not become an inferred aggression-state change, network bug, or generic E506 behavioral defect. The remaining collision/interference with same-wave SilverSpiders was addressed at encounter timing/identity level after a later parity correction showed the delayed companion should be gray02A, not the initially suspected gray02B.

For traceability, the 14 retained R33_RUNTIME_TEST_LEDGER JSON files are cumulative revision snapshots of one DM001 investigation, not 14 independent native-game experiments. A reproducible field-level comparison against the final R53 snapshot found that the earlier records mostly preserve identical scientific payloads; the changing fields include version, current authority, active test queue, next decisions and some earlier diagnostic result/status objects. A lineage matrix is retained in data/R164_DM001_CUMULATIVE_RUNTIME_LEDGER_14_SNAPSHOTS.csv, but earlier failed snapshots do not supersede the accepted mission-local route solution. Keep old evidence accessible without treating each state label as a fresh gameplay law.

The complete R18–R53 diagnostic lineage without double-counting tests

The earlier R18/R19 broad runtime ledgers overlap: both carry 19 test IDs, and R19 refines the K first-tick ejection observation. R20 then gives eight DM001-specific cases, with K failure, minimal N/O positives, failed E503/E617 and BigGreyBoss substitutions, an inconclusive post-init warp and a full-AC/route negative. R25 retains those records and adds a runtime-negative isolated humanoid_climb_setting=[6.0,20.5] test. R27/R28 add prepared delayed-route candidates and the native RM091 asynchronous Factor_Wait(0.1) precedent; their preparation must not be counted as runtime success. R29 and R32 are principally static/planning records, not collections of newly performed runtime tests.

The following R33 family consists of 14 cumulative snapshots with a strict no-removed-top-level-key lineage. Source-level diff accounting identifies each newly added diagnostic separately in data/R167_R33_14_INCREMENTAL_VERSION_DELTA.csv (which also records changed previous status/authority keys). Highlights:

  • R41–R43: changing SimpleRouteExplorer waypoint-update radius to 8.0 or 0.5 did not remove the stall; one supposedly downstream spawn hit the wrong tunnel branch. Upper/lower actors appearing stalled on the same X/Z plane did not identify a unique world-space stopping plane.
  • R43–R45: attempts to place on the wrong Y layer were corrected after scanning 257 native compressed collision shapes; R33L still stalled. This overturned a tempting roof/floor/upper-corridor inference made from X/Z alone.
  • R46–R47: R33N bypassed the initial stoop blocker; R33O accepted the frozen new spawn, but actors still took an upward detour.
  • R48–R51: R33P-A direct WP0 and R33P-B guide after WP0 failed. Only R33P-C made the native NAVMESH_H lower-branch guide the first active target; a minimal direct runtime then passed lower-route traversal, without proving full-mission completion.
  • R52–R53: full-mission observation retained route success, withdrew the earlier alleged aggro distinction, and identified wave interference. The parity sweep found that the original proposal delayed the wrong actor (gray02B). The corrected source-parity candidate delays only gray02A by ten seconds, keeping 21/22 source spawn records exact and original WP1..WP6 exact.

The final game-level practical acceptance is reported separately by later R54; the R53 parity-checked candidate status itself is not a recorded full restart/clear replay. The exact case-level precedence and inherited/not-inherited test IDs across R18/R19/R20/R25/R27/R28 are saved in data/R167_R18_R28_RUNTIME_CASE_LINEAGE.csv. This is a history of actual experiments and falsified mechanisms, not a 14-test NAV performance benchmark.
