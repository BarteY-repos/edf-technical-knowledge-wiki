EDF4.1 SK 4 m terrain and HKT/NVMD — exact source and runtime limits

ConversionMaps & NavigationOriginal EDF4.1 source terrain and native EDF6 navigation structureEDF 4.1EDF 5EDF 6
Source-backed reference · 7 SHFB profiles; 2 full-domain LOW/HIGH structural map pairs; no runtime NAV

Evidence scope: Seven original EDF4.1 SK_* terrain source maps, bounded HKT/NVMD prototypes and the later full-domain structural LOW/HIGH pairs for both SK_SANGAKU01 and SK_SANGAKU02. The completed pairs passed source-reported Havok2020 binary readback but remain private, non-installable research artifacts. No user-run EDF6 gameplay/AI result was produced. This page describes game-file data and inferred structure, not an editor's release history.

Three distinct terrain-related grids

Source height samples. The seven original EDF4.1 SHFB assets contain 1001 × 1001 height samples at 2 m spacing, spanning a 2000 m × 2000 m horizontal source domain. The measured samples occupy a 2 m heightfield; their values are not obstacle masks.

Terrain render/collision geometry. The audited FMB-style terrain extraction uses every second SHFB sample: a 4 m lattice with 500 × 500 = 250,000 cells and 500,000 triangle candidates. Each 4 m cell uses the observed native diagonal joining A–D, yielding A–B–D and A–D–C. Choosing the opposite diagonal alters local slopes and contact lines. Earlier source-to-target terrain work used 625 FMB render leaves and 2,500 compressed-mesh MAD collision tiles; those are different resource arrangements and must not be mistaken for HKT/NVMD streaming tiles.

Navigation streaming and agent data. A navigation HKT tile carries faces, edges, traversal metadata, graph/clearance and streaming relations. Its NVMD companion supplies separate per-tile/placement/faceData identity information. A source-mesh triangle that can be serialized into an HKT is not thereby an accepted walkable face for Ranger, Fencer, Hector, vehicles or a pathing agent. Source SHFB terrain, placed MAD objects and mission-local RMPA routes are different data owners.

Terrain-only HKT/NVMD samples: different sampling scales and different claims

Earlier coarse diagnostic samples cover the entire horizontal source field using a nominal 32 m or 64 m step. They demonstrate geometric domain coverage at a reduced level of detail, not the 4 m source surface used for native-sized terrain. Conversely, the later 4 m LOW/HIGH samples preserve native-resolution source geometry but cover small local windows, not the whole map. These are complementary, not two full mission-ready NAVs.

On the real-source 16-tile case, both LOW and HIGH HKT files measured 304,590 bytes, while their SHA-256 hashes differ. Their companion NVMD files each measured 660 bytes with an equal SHA-256. Across all bounded 2/4/16-tile tests, the number of HKT navmesh variants matched NVMD Section D tile IDs; the recorded faceData index 1 refers to a neutral Section C row (0,0,0). These are structural cross-file invariants, not a demonstration of the original game's agent meanings or route behavior. The exact output-size/hash rows are retained in data/R155_EDF41_R31_R32_SK_HEIGEN02_HKT_NVMD_STRUCTURAL_VARIANTS.csv.

In particular, an equal NVMD checksum between LOW and HIGH does not prove that LOW and HIGH agents use the same clearance, slope, step or target mask in EDF6. The source studies reused target-family LOW/HIGH profiles, without proving original 4.1 agent semantics.

Placed objects: a geometric contact is not an obstacle rule

The original source SK_HEIGEN02 MAPB record 2 refers to the placed sk_rockm01 shape; the examined MAD source body contains 172 triangles. A terrain-contact test observed 51 segments, yielding four candidate 4 m cells. They are a reproducible geometry-proposal set, not four proven impassable locations. Source actor-transform bit parity, radius erosion and blocking meaning remain unresolved. No candidate was automatically applied to NAV. Exact proposed coordinates are in data/R155_EDF41_SK_HEIGEN02_ROCK_CONTACT_FOUR_CANDIDATES.csv.

The same caution applies to all the original seven-map intersections described in the terrain–object contact census: a road, bridge, water surface, wall or rock may touch the ground, but its role for a specific agent requires native collision and pathing semantics, not just intersecting triangles. A contour is not by itself an off-mesh link or a passable corridor.

Why EDF5's existing navigation research matters — and where it stops

EDF5 already supplied a well-researched Havok-2015 → Havok-2020 existing-HKT migration path: deserialize a source NAV HKT and encode the target HKT/NVMD types, streaming and record layout. Original EDF4.1 SK map sources in this corpus do not contain NAVMESH.HKT, so there is no legacy NAV payload to migrate directly. Reusable EDF5/EDF6 knowledge therefore consists of the target HKT/NVMD serialization family, streaming seam identity, graph/clearance byte layout and validations, not an existing EDF4.1 walkable surface.

This is also distinct from the independent HillyCity R1D exact-oracle reconstruction: R1C/R1D target hashes, 4,971 footprints and source-cached-AABB classification address a separate candidate/bake; they do not prove whole-map SK NAV nor supersede these source terrain measurements. See R1D source authority and HKT/NVMD migration versus rebake.

What remains unproven

  • All seven SK terrain maps have full-resolution source-layout plans. Complete LOW/HIGH native-structured HKT/NVMD offline pairs are directly documented here for two maps; this update does not independently certify full-pair coverage for the other five. No installed, game-tested EDF6 navigation result is established by these records.
  • The other nine original EDF4.1 map families include multi-level/hilly/cave geometry and cannot be reduced to this single SHFB heightfield procedure. Their original walkable 3D surfaces and streaming portals remain open.
  • Source MAPB object transforms and the classification of real solid blockers vs traversable structures have not been qualified in an original-game/runtime side-by-side test; no full obstacle-clearance mask has been verified.
  • Agent radii, climb/step/ledge/slope rules, low/high profile semantics and interactions with mission AI and route directives remain unproven.
  • No deployed EDF6 NPC pathfinding, vehicle movement, mission restart, co-op or 138-mission MissionBundle coverage was tested here.

The tiled 4 m grid contains 64 tiles and 8,192 unmasked terrain triangles. Distinguish navmesh geometry from 3D source-object proximity

Subsequent source-MAD/MAPB studies distinguish solid interior and capsule-near-triangle evidence. The 265 placed static MAD objects on this one map have 166 closed-manifold and 99 open-surface instances by the tested welded-edge/orientation rule. This does not imply that the 166 are all actually impassable or that the 99 are safely walkable; it does not measure native Ranger, Fencer, Hector or underground pathing profiles. The fully enumerated 265-instance counts by three illustrative capsule radii appear in data/R161_EDF41_SK_HEIGEN02_265_MAD_PLACED_CAPSULE_COUNTS.csv.

The source surface query uses the original triangular A→E FMB diagonal for terrain elevation. A bilinear height approximation can disagree at local triangle edges. For closed obstacles, an exact candidate for the source Rz*Ry*Rx MAPB placement path was used; bit-exact EDF6 actor-matrix parity has not been proven. Do not turn any of these proximity observations into an installable mask without independently qualifying collision layer, agent dimensions, walkability, slope, clearance, and runtime path response.
