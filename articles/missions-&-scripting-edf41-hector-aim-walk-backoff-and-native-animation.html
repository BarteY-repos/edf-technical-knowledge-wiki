EDF4.1 Hector AI on an EDF6 Berserker — attack selection, back-off, aiming and gait

ConversionMissions & ScriptingMissionsEDF 4.1EDF 6
Technical reference · Exact-build class RE in retained source comments; historical M113 observation, not a new runtime test

Why model-and-gun replacement is not enough

The studied EDF4.1 Hector was mapped onto an EDF6 Berserker Large base class. Both have arms and weapons, but the two native movement, target-aim and animation controllers are not semantically equivalent. A Hector can look correct while walking sideways or firing through a pose that no longer matches its weapon muzzle. The R10 HF4 R9 source contains a class-specific bridge for actors explicitly marked by edf41_hector; ordinary EDF6 Berserkers pass through unchanged.

The following are bounded native-class findings, drawn from exact-build EDF4.1/EDF6 binary analysis embodied in the source. They are not a claim that every game/patch version shares the same addresses or that the historical plugin received fresh runtime qualification here.

Weapon choice depends on each arm's effective range

The original selection procedure runs on a firing interval of 300 + rand(300) frames, and compares the target's horizontal XZ distance with the per-arm hector_weapon_range.

  • Neither arm in range: do not start a battery.
  • Exactly one arm in range: use that arm.
  • Both in range: sometimes fire both, otherwise choose an arm randomly. Two arms of the same weapon class can fire together according to the source's distinct check.

Selecting guns from a single combined maximum range can fire a short-range weapon at a target only the long-range arm can reach. The bridge picks the arm mask once per attack and starts only the eligible batteries.

Back-off as a stateful timer, not a pathfinding order

A source Hector facing a target moves along its own forward axis, or backwards along the same axis while continuing to face the target. It does not pick an arbitrary lateral waypoint. The captured source configuration defaults to:

hector_ai_setting = [720, 1440, 540, 720, 150, 20, 80]
                    [dwell_base, dwell_rand,
                     retreat_base, retreat_rand,
                     near_range, stop_min, stop_max]

When within 150 units, a dwell counter decreases 1 per frame outside its chosen stop radius or 2 per frame inside it; at zero the actor retreats for 540 + rand(720) frames and begins a fresh cycle. It may stop and turn within a random stop radius of approximately 20–80 units. The source's walk command is ±1 in its facing axis; the smoothed native velocity corresponds to approximately 6 world units per second in the examined configuration, both forwards and backwards.

By contrast, the donor Berserker's follow state can walk toward a slot beside the target while facing it, causing lateral slide. Historical 2026-10-18 M113 observation reported 43% of walking frames more than 11° off facing; one actor appeared to walk against a wall for about 140 seconds. Those are recorded observations of that test, not universal statistics for Hectors.

Separate four operations that look like one animation

Body steering points the Hector toward the target; movement is applied along the body's current facing. Gait selection is based on the actual change in yaw and eased local velocity, rather than the command state alone. Arm aiming rotates the weapon-bearing arm bones and all dependent muzzle/world matrices.

The source gait decision distinguishes stand, turn_l, turn_r, walk_f, walk_b, walk_l, walk_r. It chooses turning if angular change dominates local movement, otherwise selects the larger local velocity axis. Walk and turn playback rates follow velocity and yaw changes; source parameters named v_walk_animation_speed and v_turn_animation_speed are applied at this layer. Replacing them with the target Berserker's generic stand/walk state can make a stationary turning Hector rotate without playing a turning animation.

For an attacking arm, the target is transformed to the arm's frame and the desired angles are approximately:

pitch = -atan2(local_y, sqrt(local_x² + local_z²))
yaw   =  atan2(local_x, local_z)

The source constrains pitch to [-π/2, 0.4π], has mirrored per-side yaw limits (0.6π gives up, 0.7π clamps) and eases 10% toward the target each frame while attacking. Outside an attack the angles relax by multiplying by 0.95 per frame. The target Berserker's own gun controller adjusts a shot aim point, but does not automatically rotate the Hector's arm bone and dependent muzzle geometry the same way.

The retained bridge therefore updates local arm rotation, world matrices of descendants, relevant weapon clips, charge/death presentation and the dedicated head CAS player in a measured order. Without the world rebuild, the visible arm may move but projectiles can still originate from stale muzzle transforms. Such hooks must be exact-build guarded; they are not portable numeric patch offsets.

Integration and evidence boundaries

The object metadata for the source-specific behaviour contains two arm ranges, a same-weapon flag and seven AI timing parameters. A state cache must not key only on a reused object address: the source compares live control-block identity and the edf41_hector marker to avoid carrying a dead actor's retreat and aim state into a new one.

This is actor AI and animation parity. It does not repair donor-map NAV geometry, mission route semantics, source-only Havok conversion or missing physical shields. Verify each independently before attributing an AI failure to this controller.
