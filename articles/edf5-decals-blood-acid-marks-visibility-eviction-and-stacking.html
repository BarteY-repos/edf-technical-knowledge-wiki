EDF5 decals: blood, acid, bullet marks, lifetime, stacking and view culling

EDF 5Game technical referencesEffects and rendering
Technical reference · Source-declared grades; this edition does not add native game runtime testing

EDF5 uses a DecalManager and several DecalBase-derived objects for splashes, projected stains, bullet marks and explosion marks. These are world-space render volumes, not paint permanently stored on map geometry. Their removal rules explain why a mark can vanish long before the nominal lifespan expires.

Different decal families

Native class Typical effect
Decal_Splash Ground or death-pool-like aligned decal
Decal_SplashProjection Hit blood or acid projected into world geometry
Decal_BulletMark Bullet impact mark, including glowing/smouldering transition
Decal_ExplosionMarks Blast/smoulder marks
FullScreen_Rain, Dust, Fog Fullscreen classes in the broader decal family, not identical to a wall mark

The studied constructors receive an InitParam with lifetime and strength. DecalBase separately tracks age, time spent undrawn, fade/dissolve and bounds. The reported ordinary duration is 20 seconds, but that number is not a guarantee a particular decal remains visible for 20 seconds.

The rules that remove marks early

  1. Out of view: the examined update logic deletes a decal after approximately one second not drawn, irrespective of the remaining nominal lifetime. The researcher verified this effect in game by changing the comparisons and seeing decals persist much longer.
  2. Global capacity: the ordinary pool has a limit of roughly 100 managed decals, with eviction when more arrive.
  3. Stacking/overlap: about 6 overlapping marks can cause fading and 8 can cause removal in the observed configuration. A new bullet mark can thus evict an older blood stain on the same wall. This overlap behavior was also tested by the source investigator.
  4. Normal age and fading: an older decal loses importance and ultimately ends its own lifetime, with special glow/fade curves for some mark classes.

The manager ranks living candidates using a factor of remaining lifetime, decal strength and time undrawn. It compares general and overlapping candidates before deciding what to demote or remove. The source notes that one hard-removal path may unlink a decal from the manager without proving immediate visual destruction; that distinction remains unverified.

Projection and attachment

The hit-blood spawner raycasts forward and creates a perspective-projected volume only after finding a surface. Its ray reach, projection depth and weight are partly linked, which means changing range can change stain appearance as well as distance. The acid spawner follows a different, simpler distance/depth path. A decal is registered in world space and is not dynamically attached to the specific wall chunk or enemy limb it struck. Consequently, a building fragment moving or disappearing does not imply the decal will move with it.

Performance and evidence

Each decal is submitted individually, and inserting one requires considering the manager's tracked set. High decal counts therefore have real CPU and rendering costs; the game does not treat 10,000 projected marks as an ordinary budget. The contributor reported a test in which many decals survived after changing removal rules, but that is not proof of normal performance or memory safety at such counts.
