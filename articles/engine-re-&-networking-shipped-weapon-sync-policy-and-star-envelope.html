Shipped weapon sync policy and star-dependent envelope

EDF 6Engine RE & NetworkingGameplay & AI
Source-backed research / case study · Exact native binary + exhaustive shipped-data policy scan

How exact RNG and receiver reconstruction fit into this policy

The firing policy determines which state is transmitted before a projectile is built. For the exact per-Weapon LCG, random-number consumption per update/shot, subtype1 cumulative counters, Charge/PreCharge state and the affected projectile classes, see Weapon RNG and batched projectile reconstruction. This is not resolved by a change in star policy alone: successful count reconciliation can leave class-internal projectile state different.

Static SGO fire_model_direction values also do not exhaust effective modes: VehicleSwingShoot's dynamic mode 2 is selected at runtime even though the qualified shipping configuration scan contains no static mode-2 entries.

EDF6 does not use one universal fire-synchronization policy for all weapons. Binary RE plus the shipped vanilla weapon corpus recovers a concrete policy surface on Weapon and shows that some weapons can change synchronization mode as their star-scaled parameters change.

Core fields and direct-fire modes

Recovered fields:

  • Weapon+0x153C — fire_sync_policy_flags;
  • Weapon+0x1540 — sync_level, with normal shipping values -1, 0, or 1.

For the direct subtype0 path, the three sync_level values have distinct authority:

sync_level Direct subtype0 authority
-1 receiver-local full transform
0 sender orientation + receiver-local translation
1 full sender transform

These modes are part of the initial weapon-fire path. They are not the same thing as the later NetworkBullet selector0/1/2 trajectory/contact publications.

Subtype1 is a different creation policy

Final policy bit 4 selects a six-Weapon-update batched subtype1 path with deferred receiver-local projectile realization.

Important differences from direct subtype0:

  • subtype1 does not carry the sender muzzle transform in the same form;
  • no active pre-spread Weapon+0xBC8 RNG-seed resynchronization is present on this path;
  • subtype1 uses the non-retained lower fire lane in the R10 policy reconstruction;
  • later NetworkBullet selectors may still publish trajectory/contact state, but they cannot retroactively recreate omitted class-internal creation state.

This distinction explains why “the projectile is networked later” is not proof that the initial projectile construction was deterministic on both peers.

Exact shipped-data scan

The R10 shipped-data closure scanned the user-supplied vanilla weapon corpus:

  • 1,839 SGO entries;
  • 1,833 individual weapon/config DSGO files;
  • 1,564 WEAPONTABLE rows;
  • 1,315 active WEAPONTABLE configurations with non-empty AmmoClass.

The recovered MAB fire_model_direction distribution in those configs was:

  • mode0: 1,708 records;
  • mode1: 125 records;
  • mode2: 0 static records;
  • parse failures: 0.

Every reachable subtype1 state in the qualified WEAPONTABLE star envelope uses normal-parser mode0. Static fire_model_direction is therefore not a substitute for evaluating the actual synchronization policy.

WSLRF / star evaluator

EDF+0xD7CE0 is the common seven-value Weapon WSLRF/star-scaled parameter evaluator used by the policy-relevant fields.

Where the result is assigned to an integer field, the recovered rule is round to nearest, half away from zero after evaluation. Confirmed consumers include:

  • AmmoCount → Weapon+0x248;
  • ReloadTime → Weapon+0x20C;
  • FireCount → Weapon+0x368;
  • FireInterval → Weapon+0x36C;
  • FireAccuracy → Weapon+0x378 as float.

Do not replace this with truncation, banker's rounding, or a guessed linear star formula.

Correlated reachable policy envelope

Across the 1,315 active configs, roughly 2.9 million correlated reachable star states were evaluated.

Stable policy groups:

  • 669 always direct sync1;
  • 162 always direct sync0;
  • 230 always direct sync-1;
  • 157 always subtype1.

Additionally:

  • 97 weapons have star-dependent policy behavior;
  • 172 can reach subtype1 in at least one state;
  • 15 switch between subtype1 and direct fire depending on reachable star values.

The 15 switchers include Dunkel N202, Penetrator / Penetrator F, Buster Shot, Double Lance, Twin Lance, several Lightning Bow variants, Raijin and Raijin α.

Implementation consequences

A desync probe or repair must log the evaluated runtime policy, not infer it only from weapon name or base config.

At minimum capture:

  1. weapon/config identity and AmmoClass;
  2. Weapon+0x153C and Weapon+0x1540;
  3. direct subtype0 versus subtype1 selection;
  4. applicable +0x1544/+0x1548/+0x154C/+0x1550 policy fields;
  5. shot-transform provenance/freshness;
  6. local/remote owner role and Soldier/Weapon SceneObject ordering.

Boundary: this article describes the initial fire-policy envelope. Later NetworkBullet selector reliability and contact semantics are separate layers and must not be conflated with initial fire authority.

Exact same-weapon policy test

A concrete falsifiable example of star-dependent policy is AWEAPON104.SGO (Penetrator F): the correlated source star states {0:0,2:0,4:0,5:3}, {0:0,2:0,4:0,5:0}, and {0:0,2:1,4:0,5:0} are predicted to select respectively subtype1/sync-1, direct/sync0, and direct/sync1. This follows the exact shipped-data evaluator and does not establish runtime acceptance of those states. See four-weapon discriminator matrix for comparison controls and logging sites.

Complete list and star-dependent examples

The 15 actual named subtype1/direct policy switchers and source-evaluated correlated star examples are now tabulated in the dedicated EDF6 fire-policy switcher reference, with an optional filterable CSV of all 1,315 active configuration records in the public archive. Do not interpret the 97 star-dependent configurations as 97 subtype1/direct switchers: 82 vary only among direct synchronization modes, while 15 cross the subtype1 boundary.
