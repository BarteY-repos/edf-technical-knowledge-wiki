EDF6 physics: mass distribution, inertia, wheels and suspension

EDF 6Game technical referencesPhysics
Technical reference · Original source reports static binary/file analysis, with separately labelled contributor in-game tests; no new tests this release

EDF6's Havok collision shape is only one part of rigid-body response. A body can have a mass value, a separate stored hknpRefMassDistribution and a set of motion properties. Vehicles then apply their own aggregate mass and wheel/suspension settings on top of that body-level description.

Stored mass and inertia

A census of OBJECT and WEAPON found 2,052 null massDistribution references among 4,251 bodies. With a null entry, the studied native engine can derive center of mass and inertia from the collision shape. A non-null hknpRefMassDistribution stores centerOfMassAndVolume and principal axes/inertia, with inertia expressed per unit mass. Changing bodyCinfo.mass therefore changes the effective inertial response even when the distribution record itself is unchanged.

The original investigator found that stored mass distributions often differ materially from naive shape-based calculations: on the surveyed population, center-of-mass offsets had a roughly 2.5 cm median discrepancy and inertias about 45% at median, although volume matched. That is a description of the research corpus, not a recommended multiplier or evidence of a file bug.

In their gameplay tests, changing shapes, friction, damping, joint limits or mass affected collisions and ragdoll dynamics. By contrast, SHKT's saved collision layer was overwritten by the game, and a stored gravityFactor often did not act as a universal per-body gravity setting. Some shell-casing and subsystem-exclusion experiments remained inconclusive. Native files contain both inertia factor 1.0 (often newer E6xx assets) and 1.5 (many inherited EDF5 assets); both were observed functioning in the contributor's tests.

What a vehicle does with SHKT masses

car_base does not simply use a sum of SHKT body masses unchanged. It takes those as relative weights, normalizes them to the SGO's car_base_all_rigid_body[0] total vehicle mass, then applies per-body factors from car_base_rigid_body.

Wheel rows carry: cast mode (0 ray, 1 cylinder), wheel attachment offset, suspension length, length addend, spring strength, two damping fields, radius and half-width. The studied suspension force is approximately:

[F_{\mathrm{spring}} = k_{\mathrm{spring}},M_{\mathrm{vehicle}},x]

where x is compression. Consequently a simple stationary compression estimate is g / total spring strength, roughly 9.8 / total strength, in the source's chosen convention. This is a model-level relation, not a guaranteed observed compression for every moving vehicle or contact geometry. Under-soft suspensions can let the chassis intersect climbing obstacles or be thrown off; the exact effect depends on the wheel and hull shapes.

Tested versus unresolved

The investigator reports normal vehicle behavior for a rewritten V601 tank's compressed meshes and reports standing on a newly added invisible collision plate. These are contributor-verified game tests. By contrast, the hypothesis that very thin/open compressed triangle meshes cause vehicle sinking and shaking is unproven, so no root cause is asserted here.
