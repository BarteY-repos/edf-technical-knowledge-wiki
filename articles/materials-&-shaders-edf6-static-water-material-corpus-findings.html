EDF6 native static-water material: observed shader and resource family

EDF 6Materials & ShadersShaders
Technical reference · Four-map retained native MDB shader/resource census

At a glance

What it is: The sampled EDF6 map materials include a native static-water shader family with multiple sampler and parameter roles.

Where it fits: The positive native sample comes from NW_EUROPE01; related notes include externally reported water pixel paths but with different evidence scope.

Important distinction: One confirmed snd_BRDF_Map_Water material does not establish universal River/Ocean variants or exact final-frame water equations.

The native map/model material shader family snd_BRDF_Map_Water is present in the inspected EDF6 map RAB corpus. The available evidence supports a static authored-water material profile and a set of texture inputs; it does not establish separate general-purpose River and Ocean shader variants.

Cross-generation boundary: the independently analyzed EDF5 water shader is SND_MAP_WATER_TRANSPARENT, a forward material with scene-view-position and global-environment-cubemap samplers. Its exact source-reconstructed reflection LOD uses the roughness multiplied by the environment mip count. The retained EDF6 R18 Water shader instead establishes a roughness-dependent reflection factor shaped like max(0, 1 - (1 - roughness)^2) in its studied family. Even when both games draw a transparent-looking water surface, the two shader names, cbuffer layouts, sampling and reflection rules are not a portable common ABI. See the EDF5 water shader's measured passes, bindings and alpha behavior; the two reported EDF6 pixel paths later on this page retain their different, weaker evidence status.

Authoring parameters in the bounded profile

The recovered experimental static-water exporter exposes a measured profile incorporating overall color, specular, roughness and metallic parameters, blending/depth controls, UV scale/scroll settings and separate bubble/normal-layer controls. The authoring representation contains two bubble layers and three normal-related controls (amount, scroll X/Z and UV scale) in that examined profile. This is a description of recovered source-owned authoring controls; do not reinterpret it as proof of the complete native shader algorithm.

The material is applied to a bounded horizontal surface with UV0 and separately specified planar/collision assumptions. A valid MDB/RAB/MAPB build is not enough to demonstrate correct transparency order, depth blending, shader time behavior, culling or gameplay water interaction.

Native Water MDB: exact typed parameter contract

The retained NW_EUROPE01 material has 26 parameter records in native order, four ordered samplers (bubble_albedo, normal_map1, normal_map2, normal_map3), material flags 3, priority 0 and layer 2. This is the serialized MDB ABI, independent of whether a shader actually reads every field in one rendering variant.

Importantly, bubble1_scroll_speed, bubble2_scroll_speed and all three normalN_scroll_speed records are two-component type=1, size=2 vectors. bubble_diffuse, specular_color and water_color are three-component 2/3 values; the remaining 18 are 0/1 scalars. The source does not say that an RGB material record has an implicit alpha component.

The mesh-backed sample has a 60-byte declaration: BINORMAL +0x00, TANGENT +0x08, NORMAL +0x10, POSITION +0x18, TEXCOORD0 +0x20, BLENDWEIGHT +0x28 and BLENDINDICES +0x38. These are native semantic offsets, not inferred HLSL vector formats. No second UV stream is present in this sampled declaration; that fact does not prevent animated UV coordinates from being constructed in a vertex shader.

In particular, the existence and names of blend_depth, bubble_depth and near_fade do not independently prove the complete depth/foam equations reported later on this page. The parameter ABI is native-source evidence; the two proposed rendering paths retain their separately stated evidence status.

What cannot be inferred

  • No separately named river/ocean shader occurred in these four retained inventories. That is a finite census, not proof that EDF6 globally has no other water implementation.
  • snd_BRDF_Map_Water name alone does not prove that every wet/reflective surface should use it.
  • A static-water authored writer being deterministic offline is not proof of in-game visual parity.
  • Avoid creating fabricated snd_BRDF_Map_River/...Ocean families without independent native material or engine evidence.

See MDB material shading, native shader-family field guide and advanced water authoring.

Native water is a dedicated map object, not just an MDB material

The inspected NW_EUROPE01 source model eu1_nw_field01_water.mdx is registered as MAPB T1 Class 10, with a 33-field water SGO (Type="水", Collision_Enable=-1, Durability=0, CrashType=0, FenceType=-1). Its MAPO slot references a visual MDB, _hk.mdb and .hkt; there are no p58 destruction groups or T50 pieces. The native MAD contains a static water collision body with 10 triangles that matches the ten-triangle planar visual footprint. This distinguishes resource identity, shader ABI and collision shape from any unproved fluid gameplay. See native EDF6 class-10 water object.

Two externally reported EDF6 water pixel paths: depth/foam and simple

The 17 November 2026 water-shader interpretation describes two rendering behaviors, not two independently observed native MDB shader names. Both reportedly shade after composition through a forward color output. Native EDF6 R18 static inspection supports a Water pixel shader writing SV_TARGET0, but does not establish the complete frame order, blend state or which of the two described variants is bound to a given water object.

The shared reported path combines three normal textures sampled at v8.xy, v8.zw and v9.xy, with independent normal1_amount, normal2_amount, and normal3_amount. The stronger, hash-pinned R12 Water reconstruction decodes packed X from R×A, Y from G, reconstructs Z, normalizes each strength-adjusted layer, sums layers and transforms through the tangent/binormal/normal basis. The external description's R×B conflicts with that pinned Water shader; R×B is documented for FieldRender terrain. Do not substitute one for the other without matching the shader variant and source channels.

The reported bubble/depth path reads previously drawn scene view-Z at the water pixel and compares it with the water surface's view-Z, creating depth-opacity and foam-fade curves. Two bubble_albedo samples use v9.zw and v10.xy; foam blends a bubble-derived color, reduces metallic contribution and moves roughness toward 1. Reported output alpha is max(foam, depthBlend) × saturate(v3.y), with an additional near-camera fade applied to depthBlend. The exact view-position resource name and near_fade parenthesis/order are unresolved in the supplied expression, so the cleaned pseudocode is not claimed to be native bytecode.

The external depth/foam description can be summarized in conceptual equations, not native HLSL transcription:

d = abs(sceneViewZ - waterViewZ)
depthBlend = min(pow(d / blend_depth, blend_curve), 1)
bubbleDepthFade = min(pow(d / bubble_depth, bubble_curve), 1)
bubble = sample(bubble_albedo, v9.zw) + sample(bubble_albedo, v10.xy)
foam = saturate(bubble.a * 0.5 - bubbleDepthFade)
alpha = max(foam, depthBlend * nearFade) * saturate(v3.y)

The reported near-camera factor reaches zero at abs(waterViewZ) = near_fade and one by 2 × near_fade for positive near_fade. The supplied code has an unmatched parenthesis and ambiguous placement of division relative to saturate; this descriptive interval is not an exact instruction-order proof.

The reported simple path skips scene-depth and bubble sampling. It uses water_color.rgb, metallic/roughness and the shared lighting, with alpha derived from water_color.a, camera near_fade and v3.y. Both paths reportedly apply directional/ambient/point light, shadows, environment reflections and two-stage brightness-fog then fog processing using FOG_FADE input channels v3.z and v3.x. Exact branch formulas, pass state, draw assignment and visual results still need native confirmation.

The supplied fog order first raises RGB to a brightness-fog floor using pow(saturate(v3.z), brightness_fog_curve) × brightness_fog_color.a, then blends toward fog_color.rgb using pow(saturate(v3.x), fog_curve) × fog_color.a. It reportedly consumes the incoming FOG_FADE channels without recomputing distance with fog_mul/fog_add. Native R5 supports bounded Water RGB dependence on the fog fields; the full two-stage arithmetic and input provenance still need shader-stage confirmation. The simple path's reported direct-specular roughness floor is 0.04, another variant-specific claim not established by the four-map material-name census.

Pinned Water pixel shader: resource registers and normal arithmetic

The native main snd_BRDF_Map_Water PS with SHA-256 e1c1ad37230656dc50dc31fc613002fae6db3460a6441c380c5f9e5bd80ef21a is independently qualified through its DXBC reflection and instruction stream. These are actual shader register/byte-offset contracts, not the similarly named MDB serialization order.

All three packed normal samples have the native sample-operand (R,G,A,B) swizzle. Each layer reconstructs nx=2*(R*A-0.5), ny=2*(G-0.5), nz=sqrt(max(1-nx²-ny²,1e-5)) (the safeguard has float32 bits 0x3727C5AC). XY is scaled by its own normalN_amount before separate layer normalization; the source instruction order forms L2 + L1 + L3, then transforms using the normalized T/B/N basis and normalizes the result. This is different from the independently proven FieldRender R×B, inverted-G decode.

The RDEF descriptor uses dimension code 4 for native Texture2D while the dcl_resource token uses its own enum value 3 for the same resource type. The reflected shader return type FLOAT (5) describes shader sampling, not the disk DDS compression format. The environment binding uses RDEF dimension code 10 (TextureCubeArray), not an ordinary Texture2D or universal global cubemap.

Other native Water pixel-stage resource declarations: bubble_albedo is at t5/s3, g_xgl_viewXYZ_id_texture at t7, and g_xgl_shadow_depth at t8. The latter two do not have corresponding named sampler registers in the R18 resource-binding union. The full native Water PS union is thus t2–t8 and its named samplers s0–s4, not merely the four sampler records serialized in the sampled Water MDB. Declaration alone does not prove that a selected runtime branch samples the view-position or shadow resource, nor does it prove a complete foam/depth equation. See the four-family register matrix.

Additional RDEF pixel-stage xgl_user_param byte offsets from the same pinned Water family: water_color.rgb at +4/+8/+12, all_scroll_speed at +36 and near_fade at +148. These are offsets in the GPU constant-buffer parameter record, not the positions of ordered MDB parameter records. A native Water vertex-shader path separately consumes scroll/normal fields to construct UV motion; none of these scalar names by itself proves the full final alpha expression or exact shader time source.

Pinned Water environment index, reflection direction and LOD

The Water PS input signature identifies v1.xyz as EYE_VECTOR, v4.xyz as NORMAL, v5.xyz as BINORMAL, v6.xyz as TANGENT, and v7.x as a uint32 ENV_INDEX. Instruction 402 converts that integer with UTOF to the fourth texture coordinate, i.e. a cube-array layer selector rather than another UV coordinate. Instructions 116–121 form the normalized reflection direction equivalent to normalize(E - 2*dot(E,N)*N) from the normalized eye vector E and perturbed normal N.

Native instruction 115 computes r1.w = prior_r1.w*(1-roughness)+roughness (roughness at xgl_user_param +0x20). Instructions 400–406 then build the symbolic environment LOD RESINFO(t6,mip0).w × max(1-(1-r1.w)²,0) and sample t6/s4 using SAMPLE_L. The actual runtime RESINFO.w result, SRV mip range, selected array contents, draw-time variant and whole-frame blending are not recovered from static DXBC. The Water PS has a bounded terminal SV_TARGET0 color/alpha path and brightness-fog dependencies, but that does not independently prove the two externally reported water variants or their draw order.
