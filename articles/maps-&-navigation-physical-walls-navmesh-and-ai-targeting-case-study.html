AI targeting through walls — collision versus navigation

UniversalMaps & NavigationNavigationEDF 5EDF 6
Technical reference · Runtime-negative experiment / exact source geometry

A monster firing at an NPC through a solid wall does not, by itself, prove that the wall's collision mesh is missing. EDFROOM/DM019 gave a useful real-game demonstration of why EDF's physical collision, navigation data, target selection, line-of-sight checks and projectile collision must be examined as separate systems.

The observed behavior

The EDF5-derived EDFROOM mission was brought to a state where the wall physically existed in EDF6: actors could not pass through it, and spider web projectiles collided with it. However, in a particular RoomB area some spiders still aimed and fired toward allied NPCs on the other side. The observed effect was localized, especially evident for spider-to-NPC interactions, and should not be generalized to every enemy or map.

That observation establishes a narrow fact:

Layer What the observation supports What it cannot prove
Collision (MAD / Havok) The wall blocks actors and tested projectiles All collision shapes/material flags are native-exact
Projectile physics The tested webs hit/stop at the wall Enemy AI checked the wall before selecting a target
AI perception/targeting Spiders attempted attacks toward occluded NPCs Which specific perception/LOS/agent query returned the wrong result
NAVMESH HKT/NVMD Potential route and graph inputs worth investigating A missing walkable face or edge is necessarily the cause

The map's lighting and wall-mounted enemy-spawner orientation were separate mission-script issues, not explanations for a physically missing wall. The 2001 → CreateEnemy2 mapping was runtime-confirmed for the spawner context.

Correct structure is not the same as correct gameplay

Several seemingly promising file-format fixes were real structural corrections but did not fix the AI symptom:

  • The correspondence between HKT face data and NVMD Section C was corrected, yet the targeted runtime check was negative.
  • Source-derived static AABB trees were checked across 10 low and 10 high tiles. Leaves covered their intended faces, nodes were reachable and no missing/duplicate face-tree relationships explained the symptom.
  • Incorrect research decoding of narrow integers and hkHalf16 values caused earlier absurd graph/clearance interpretations. Correcting the decoder repaired the analysis, not the game.
  • An early hypothesis of a simple missing ~1.75 m RoomB boundary was superseded after separating physical overlap, internal seams and streaming geometry.

This is a reproducible lesson for any map converter: a parser invariant may be valuable even when the behavior under investigation remains unchanged.

A real streaming connection passed through solid geometry

The audited high navmesh had a connection between section UIDs 2 and 3, joining (face 24, edge 74) to (face 195, edge 735) near RoomB. The shortest bridge between the relevant edges measured about 1.114206 m, of which approximately 1.108306 m lay inside the closed convex wall collider — 99.47% penetration.

A conservative, source-derived detector identified a bridge only if:

solid penetration length >= 0.05 m
AND
penetration length / bridge length >= 0.90
AND
source collision binding is a supported closed convex shape

It uses source HKT + source MAD/MAC spatial context and rejects ambiguous cases. It must not treat every intersection with a surface mesh/MOPP as a valid inside/outside test: a mesh surface does not necessarily represent a closed solid volume.

The detector selected three analogous source relations in its audited scope (DM019 high, IG_EDFROOM01 low, IG_EDFROOM02 high) and none in the listed CAVE503, HEIGEN507, NW_EUROPE01, KAIGAN502, SANGAKU506 and DESERT508 controls. This is offline consistency evidence, not a general algorithm for generating EDF6 streaming links.

The decisive negative runtime experiment

A narrowly scoped diagnostic removed exactly that one high-HKT connection and its two AABB records. The serialized difference was 80 bytes: one 16-byte connection plus two 32-byte AABBs. The low HKT, ordinary navmesh faces/edges, graph, static tree, clearance and NVMD were kept unchanged.

The later in-game observation was negative: spiders still aimed/fired at allied NPCs through/toward the wall, while their web projectiles struck the barrier. Therefore the crossed-solid connection was a genuine structural anomaly but not a demonstrated root cause of that AI symptom. It should not be silently removed from every map as a proposed gameplay fix.

What is still unknown

The responsible AI query/target-visibility or LOS rule was not resolved. It may depend on the target type, agent class, route query or state inside the engine. None of these possibilities has yet been uniquely demonstrated. The low/high HKT variants are not proven to map one-to-one to spider-versus-other-enemy navigation; their observed erosionRadius values do not justify that claim.

How to debug similar issues

  1. Verify physical blocking separately: actors, projectiles and the relevant wall surfaces.
  2. Observe who targets whom (enemy→player versus enemy→NPC), not only where projectiles stop.
  3. Test mission creation/spawner orientation independently from map geometry.
  4. Validate HKT/NVMD face identity, graph, streaming, static trees and clearance with corrected decoders.
  5. Use one-variable runtime tests to decide whether a structural repair changes actual AI behavior.
  6. Only then investigate AI perception, LOS, navigation query and target selection in the exact engine build.

EDFROOM's IG_EDFROOM01/02 map family did not have FMB; collision/navigation investigation used HKT/NVMD/MAD/MAC, with models in other resource containers. Don't invent an FMB dependency for these maps.

Evidence boundary: collision/projectile observations and the negative runtime experiment are gameplay evidence; the crossing detector and byte/geometry measurements are offline/source evidence. The cause of AI-versus-NPC wall targeting remains unresolved.

See HKT/NVMD compatibility versus native rebake, MAD collision contracts and map formats.

Measured physical geometry and graph controls

The converted EDFROOM collision corpus preserved 297,951 logical triangles after a documented degeneracy filter, and its 101 compressed meshes passed a bounded shape-key/broadphase check (166,084 expected and reachable unique keys). More importantly, 20 examined wall/corridor records retained zero shared-section seams despite source-to-target coordinate quantization. The measured candidate still showed correct projectile/actor blocking in the user's runtime observation. These facts reinforce the distinction between physically present walls and AI targeting, but do not demonstrate global collision exactness. See EDFROOM MAD physical collision case.

The DM019 coarse inter-cluster graph contained exactly 84 LOW and 374 HIGH edges derived from current face adjacency, without extra/missing edges. A source hkHalf16 decoding error had previously made narrow costs appear inconsistent; correcting the decoder repaired analysis only, not the actual AI behavior. See HKT graph/cache decoding.

R176 — observed enemy-to-ally asymmetry, without claiming a new AI rule

The original user observation for the EDFROOM/DM019 spider room supplies more context than a generic "can attack through walls" statement:

  • When spiders were targeting the player, they were not reported to shoot the player through the wall. The anomaly was specifically noticed when they aimed/fired webs toward allied NPCs behind it.
  • Spiders could move toward the obstruction and accumulate by a room corner; one spider then turned toward the player after noticing them, switching from its previous apparent allied-NPC target.
  • During the later R2O high-only shortcut-removal runtime test, several spiders still aimed and fired toward the obstructed allied NPCs. The observer did not notice allies firing back in that particular observation. This is not a universal statement that friendly AI never targets spiders across walls.
  • The webs still hit the physical wall. Neither these observations nor the negative R2O experiment demonstrates a missing MAD wall, projectile pass-through, broken AI collision for every target, or a specific defective line-of-sight function.

This narrows the diagnostic evidence to target-type-dependent AI perception/selection and possibly agent-specific path/LOS queries. It does not establish which function or native query returned an inappropriate result. Evidence: EDFROOM_AI_WALL_RUNTIME_OBSERVATION_2026-08-25.json (original gameplay observation) and EDFROOM_R2O_RUNTIME_NEGATIVE_2026-08-26.json (subsequent negative one-variable gameplay experiment). The test took place in a modified EDF5-derived mission running in EDF6, not as a verified defect in a clean retail mission.
