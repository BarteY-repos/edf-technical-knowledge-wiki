EDF6 weapon RNG, batched fire and remote projectile reconstruction

EDF 6Engine RE & NetworkingGameplay & AI
Technical reference · Exact EDF6 binary analysis, qualified vanilla weapon corpus; network fix not runtime proven

At a glance

What this page answers: EDF6 can send direct weapon fire or reconstruct a batch on the receiver; matching shot count does not guarantee identical random spread or projectile state.

How to read it: Separate weapon RNG, per-projectile RNG, batched-fire selection and NetworkBullet data before diagnosing a visible desync.

Evidence boundary: Addresses refer to a pinned EDF.dll build. The static analysis and proposed checks are not universal proof of remote gameplay synchronization.

A remote weapon shot is not necessarily a copy of a sender-owned projectile. EDF6 supports direct fire and batched fire with receiver-local reconstruction; the latter may reproduce the number of shots without reproducing all of their original random or class-internal state. This distinction is important when investigating mismatched firing direction, lightning paths, flame motion or explosion timing.

The offsets below belong to the analyzed EDF6 EDF.dll build (SHA-256 prefix 0d5092393a1b…). They are observations of that build, not portable structure offsets for every release.

The weapon random-number generator

The process has a global RNG state. Construction of a new Weapon derives its live seed from an advance of that global state. The Weapon instance then maintains its own 64-bit state at Weapon+0xBC8.

The recovered linear-congruential update is:

state = (state * 0x5D588B656C078965 + 0x269EC3) mod 2^64

For the analyzed common firing path, RNG consumption depends on the event:

Event Weapon RNG effect
Weapon OnUpdate One advance
FireSpreadType 1 or 3 spread step One additional random-spread advance
FireSpreadType 0, 2 or 4 No random-spread advance at this step
Each created projectile Another advance; an upper-derived value is handed to the projectile
A new Weapon instance A new seed lineage derived from process-global RNG

In the analyzed direct-fire packet policy, flag Weapon+0x153C & 0x08 causes the sender to transmit the pre-spread Weapon RNG value. The receiver restores that value before calling the shared projectile-creation logic. That is a concrete mechanism for deterministic spread when this bit is active.

Not all fire policies include that bit. A receiver calculating a shot later with a different RNG history need not reproduce the sender's original outcome.

Direct subtype0 versus batched subtype1

Property Direct subtype0 Batched subtype1
Initial transform Policy-dependent direct sender/local transform Recreated locally on receiver; no equivalent transmitted sender muzzle transform
Pre-spread RNG Can be transported/restored when the direct-policy bit is enabled No active pre-spread seed restoration in the examined Basic, Charge, PreCharge or Gatling paths
Timing Direct fire action Publication deferred for six subsequent Weapon updates after delay arm
Delivery record Direct fire may select retained/non-retained path by policy Non-retained lower fire lane
Loss behavior Depends on selected direct policy A lost final publication is not automatically retransmitted by the subtype1 sender

Subtype1 tracks a locally realized shot counter (Weapon+0xBD0) and a received cumulative target (Weapon+0x1544). A later higher target can make a receiver catch up on missed projectile-count positions, and lower received targets are rejected. This is count reconciliation, not exact replay of each historical shot.

For Charge / PreCharge, extension data is read as pending state and committed only for accepted count updates. A later cumulative counter does not reconstruct every missing intermediate charge value. Accordingly, a later reconstruction can use a newer accepted charge state, changing fire count, accuracy, speed, damage, explosion or size parameters on affected weapon paths.

Weapon configurations that can change policy with stars

A qualified scan of 1,315 active WEAPONTABLE configurations, including roughly 2.9 million correlated reachable star states, found 157 weapons always using subtype1, 172 capable of reaching subtype1, and 15 that can switch from subtype1 to direct fire depending on star-scaled values. Examples include Penetrator, Buster Shot, Double Lance, Twin Lance, Lightning Bow variants and Raijin.

A weapon's base SGO or name is therefore not sufficient to infer its active networking policy; the evaluated star parameters matter. The detailed evaluator and all policy counts are documented in weapon synchronization policies.

The six subtype1 weapons with RNG-sensitive initial spread

The complete active-weapon scan identifies six always-subtype1 weapon configurations with FireSpreadType 1 or 3. This is a bounded list of initial-spread cases, not a list of every subtype1 weapon with RNG-sensitive projectile behaviour.

Active SGO Weapon name Weapon family FireSpreadType
AWEAPON194.SGO Wide Acid Gun BasicShoot 1
MPACK_B_WEAPON016.SGO Extreme Acid Gun BasicShoot 1
PWEAPON019.SGO Phalanx U1 ChargeShoot 3
PWEAPON032.SGO Phalanx W2 ChargeShoot 1
PWEAPON068.SGO Destroyer Blaster W PreChargeShoot 1
PWEAPON117.SGO Lightning Rapid Bow T2 ChargeShoot 1

The class-method audit found no additional seed-restoring Weapon+0xBC8 writer in BasicShoot, ChargeShoot or PreChargeShoot. Gatling's class packet helper is called with its seed-serialization flag cleared. These findings apply to the analyzed four ordinary weapon-family paths; specialized Drone/SubDrone implementations are separate.

The current Weapon RNG stream can reconverge after delayed receiver catch-up when seed, update and projectile-step histories match, while the original spread sample does not: the receiver samples at the later reconstruction phase. Random spread reduces an upper-state sample into one of 10,000 buckets, so accidental equality is possible but not protocol-enforced. New Weapon construction reseeds from process-global RNG; ordinary reload does not reset the same Weapon instance to a fixed seed in the audited paths.

The projectile has its own RNG state

The initial random value for a projectile is stored at BulletBase::InitParam+0x1A0. The constructor copies it to the final live BulletBase+0x130. These are different address bases: interpreting +0x1A0 as an offset in the finished Bullet object is incorrect.

Three observed projectile classes consume random state or the resulting constructor values beyond their initial trajectory:

Projectile class RNG-dependent internal behavior What later ordinary network state does not restore
FlameBullet02 Constructor-initialized motion scalars used by OnUpdate Class motion scalars (including +0x11D0/+0x11D8)
LightningBullet01 Randomized path/current position and further state advancement Bullet RNG/path history, including Bullet+0x130
SolidExpBullet01 Randomly initialized detonation countdown; expiration launches a BlowHit01 effect Detonation countdown (+0xF00)

Of the evaluated active weapon corpus, 40 weapons can reach subtype1 while using one of these three AmmoClass families: 30 always subtype1 and 10 with star-dependent subtype1/direct switching. An additional subtlety: this problem can occur even with non-random initial FireSpreadType 0. The divergence is then caused by post-construction projectile behavior, not initial aiming spread.

Later synchronization of the current Weapon RNG value does not rewrite an already-created Bullet's historical RNG-derived constructor state. A receiver may ultimately have the right shot count and still simulate different class behavior.

What NetworkBullet can and cannot repair

EDF6 can send later projectile updates under several selector bits at Bullet+0xE8C:

Selector Transmitted class of state Consequence
0 (bit 0x2) Common spatial position/motion snapshot Can update common trajectory state, not hidden class history
1 (bit 0x1) External-object contact/reference frame Can re-anchor projectile position to a contacted object
2 (bit 0x4) Missile collision/contact record (point, normal, object identity) Can affect reflection/trajectory; only enabled by examined MissileBullet01/02 constructors

Shipping NetworkBullet selectors use Sandlot mode0 retained reliability over physically unreliable EOS P2P. Sequence/ACK/retransmission can recover serialized selector bytes after a transient physical loss. This does not create the missing Flame, Lightning or SolidExp fields, which are absent from those payloads in the examined paths.

See selector payloads and contact behavior and application-layer reliability.

How to investigate an apparent aim/shot desync

Trace these layers separately, rather than forcing the Soldier's orientation everywhere:

  1. The local and remote Weapon identity, FireSpreadType and current star-evaluated sync policy.
  2. The sender muzzle/per-shot transform and whether a direct packet transports the pre-spread RNG.
  3. Whether creation follows direct subtype0 or six-update subtype1.
  4. The Weapon+0xBD0 realized count and +0x1544 target; if Charge/PreCharge, pending versus committed extension state.
  5. The projectile's initial constructor RNG-derived state and the later class-specific consumers.
  6. Only then, NetworkBullet selector delivery, later trajectory/contact updates and Sandlot reliable-message acknowledgment.

Evidence boundary: These are exact static/native binary reconstructions for the specified build and a qualified shipped-weapon corpus. They do not demonstrate that a runtime fix for live network desynchronization has been implemented or validated. The minimum state to add to the protocol and the safest injection point remain open.

Controlled verification examples, without treating the plan as a runtime result

For a practical same-build comparison, use four source-studied cases: M2 Raven as a non-random-spread subtype1 control, MR111 Fang as full-sender direct subtype0, Penetrator F as a single weapon with three star-dependent policies, and Wide Acid Gun as a receiver-local RNG-sensitive subtype1 control. The full source-derived star-slot states, event RVAs and expected timelines are in fire-sync runtime discriminator matrix. Those traces were proposed and statically qualified as a probe, not reported as successful paired gameplay observations.

Why most RNG-sensitive projectile state is not random initial spread

The R14 shipped-data crosswalk found 40 active weapons capable of entering subtype1 with FlameBullet02, LightningBullet01 or SolidExpBullet01: 30 always subtype1 and 10 star-dependent. Their FireSpreadType distribution was 38 spread0, one spread1 and one spread2. This is a bounded intersection of those three projectile-class families with subtype1 reachability, not the same set as the six always-subtype1 weapons with RNG-sensitive initial spread discussed above.

Consequently the main post-spawn divergence mechanism in this class subset can occur even when the weapon itself fires with nonrandom spread. The sender and receiver may create the Bullet at different points in their Weapon RNG progression; the initial value is passed via BulletBase::InitParam+0x1A0 into live BulletBase+0x130. The three derived classes can then initialize flame motion, lightning path or explosive fuse time differently. Later equal shot counts or successful Sandlot ACKs do not reconstruct that missing constructor history.
