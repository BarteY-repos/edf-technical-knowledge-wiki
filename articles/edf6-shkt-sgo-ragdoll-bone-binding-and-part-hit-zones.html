EDF6 SHKT and SGO: ragdoll bones, binding tables, hit zones and weak points

EDF 6Game technical referencesPhysics
Technical reference · Original source reports static binary/file analysis, with separately labelled contributor in-game tests; no new tests this release

An EDF6 ragdoll is not self-contained in an .SHKT file. The Havok SHKT describes physical bodies, constraints and collision shapes; the accompanying SGO connects those bodies to the animated model, selects the data file, and describes many part-specific damage rules. Understanding that division is essential when examining enemy hitboxes or apparently misplaced ragdolls.

What points to the SHKT?

A game SGO refers to a physics object with a value such as ["app:/object/RagdollTest_ari_has_hka.shkt", { ... }]. The source corpus uses keys including ragdoll, body_ragdoll, armor_ragdoll, heavy_armor_ragdoll, barrier_collision, ragdoll_d, subparts and bullet_*. Multiple character-color SGOs can reuse a single physics file: ant color variants are a documented example.

The bodies are identified by their names, not solely by array position. Typical names are RagDollProxys.kosi, RagDollProxys.head or ragdoll_<part>. The engine's RagdollController::BindDependency finds the named body and associates it with an animation node; a related native lookup indexes body names using a FNV-1a hash. A contributor verified in the game that leaving an SGO reference to a removed SHKT body can crash before mission start. Renaming a body and updating the corresponding SGO references worked in their test.

Two mappings, opposite directions

SGO table Relationship Gameplay phase
ragdoll_from_animation Animated bone → physical body Following the living character's animation
animation_from_ragdoll Physical body → animated bone Displaying a physics-driven corpse

A row contains [body, bone] or [bone, body] followed by relative position, rotation and scale. Quaternion rotation combines with the parent body's orientation; position is transformed into the parent's local frame. The joint hierarchy and SHKT shapes are not a substitute for those mappings.

The source investigator compared 44,159 rows to MDB bind poses. Approximately 81% of animation_from_ragdoll and 98% of ragdoll_from_animation rows were within 1 mm and 0.5° of the bind pose. The exceptions often describe fingers, mouths or weapon bones in an authored alternative pose. These percentages apply to the surveyed SGO/MDB corpus, not every game release.

Coordinates and body-specific damage

The studied SHKT and MDB use metres, +Y up, +X to the model's left (_l parts); the physics body transform, collision-shape local coordinates and model bind pose must still be distinguished. No general XYZ axis swap is required between these two file families.

For part damage and weak spots, inspect the SGO, not only SHKT. The source includes a weak_point object with a named body, effect and separate damage multipliers (e.g. Berserker ragdoll_eye), and humanoid_base_parts_config with part names, parents, HP, damage multipliers, model nodes and SHKT body-name suffixes. The SHKT supplies the shape that gets hit; SGO decides much of the part-specific response.

Evidence boundary

The code-name lookup and corpus counts are static/binary research. Body rename, SGO mapping and missing-body effects were tested by original contributor ktaro, but have not been repeated in this wiki revision. The exact lookup RVAs 0x6E6A50, 0x6EA4B0 and 0x51E500 belong to the cited steam-678ccb46 build only. The source also notes that moving all ant bodies with corrected SGO maps largely preserved behavior, but the living hitbox's exact use of each mapping element remains unresolved.
