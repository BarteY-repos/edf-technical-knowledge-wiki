EDF6 renderer presentation versus fixed gameplay updates

EDF 6Performance & 120 FPSPerformance
Source-backed research / case study · Runtime

EDF6's simulation and its display output are not conceptually one clock. The source and runtime experiments show that a renderer can present additional visual frames without necessarily advancing enemy AI, weapons or physics more frequently.

A real camera observation

In an isolated moving-camera trial, the measured rates were:

Counter Rate
Distinct type-0 camera states 90.306 Hz
Fixed gameplay cadence 59.922 Hz
Presented frames 89.728 Hz

The test demonstrates genuinely distinct camera states above 60 per second while gameplay remains near the native cadence. It does not demonstrate a complete 120 Hz visual update of characters, foliage, particles, shadows or scripted events. Visible stutter was still reported.

Why an FPS counter alone is insufficient

A Present can reuse an earlier world/camera state. The number of Presents is therefore not the number of distinct animations. Useful independent measurements distinguish fixed simulation updates, actual Presents, unique camera transforms and when world render-state submission or character skinning is performed.

The 60 Hz gameplay step matters to reload timers, weapon cadence, collision integration, AI and networking. Increasing those systems' update counts as a side effect of extra Presents changes gameplay; it is not purely a graphics upgrade.

Visibility and asynchronous work

An experiment that reduced auxiliary visibility calculations produced black-surface flicker and delayed/coarse shadows. This illustrates a relationship between visible state, occlusion and frame ownership: rendering a new camera position against stale visibility data can create artifacts.

A worker that samples a global simulation tick is not necessarily processing the render job for that tick. Source observations favor matching local presentation state to the job actually being processed, but a general-purpose synchronized implementation has not been demonstrated by these studies.

Scope

The source contains limited successful camera measurements and separate negative tests. It does not prove full-game smooth 120 FPS, correct online behavior at that rate, or stability of every shader/VFX/skin subsystem. Native D3D11/DXGI measurements cannot be freely substituted for DXVK or another graphics path.
