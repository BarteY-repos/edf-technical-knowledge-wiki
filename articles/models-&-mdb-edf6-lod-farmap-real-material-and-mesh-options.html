LOD and FarMap — geometry, materials and shaders

EDF 6Models & MDBModels
Technical reference · Finite four-map native sample; runtime LOD switching untested

EDF6 map models may have multiple geometry levels of detail (LOD). A lower-detail MDB does not automatically require a shader whose name contains LOD. This matters when reducing polygon counts, building a loader, or converting a model: the mesh, material family and switching behavior are separate systems.

What the retained native materials show

Across the examined NW_EUROPE01, HEIGEN507, CAVE503 and DESERT508 map-resource archives, six shader names were explicitly related to LOD or FarMap:

Shader family name Observed native materials in the four-map corpus
snd_BRDF_Map_LOD 5
snd_BRDF_Map_LOD_CC 12
snd_Map_LOD 1
snd_Map_LOD_CC 15
snd_Map_LOD_NoEnv 1
snd_FarMap_Simple 5

These are measured resource occurrences, not a list of all shaders in every EDF6 asset. An _lod.mdb name is likewise a clue about a model role, not a complete material specification.

Native LOD geometry can retain an ordinary shader

In the tested DESERT508 archive there are 16 _lod.mdb resource names, and none uses the six special LOD/FarMap shaders. Most use the ordinary snd_Map_Build_NoOcc family, with ordinary Object/Build/Glass families also present. NW_EUROPE01 also includes an LOD companion using the regular snd_BRDF_Map_Common_Clip shader.

Therefore the transformation “lower-detail mesh → replace shader with snd_BRDF_Map_LOD” is not a general EDF6 rule. For native round-trips, retain the original material unless a particular paired LOD profile supports a change.

What actually changes when authoring an LOD

A typical manually authored LOD can reduce mesh complexity while preserving the normal native MDB material structure. A valid converter must still respect:

  • Vertex declaration and UV channels (including texture coordinates required for normals, macro dirt and occlusion);
  • Tangent space and skinning channels where native materials require them;
  • Material parameter type/size, ordering, sampler slots and raw values;
  • Resource identities and the engine's method of associating base and LOD assets.

A lower triangle count alone does not establish valid in-game switching. Switch thresholds, visibility, and real performance were not verified by this particular four-map material census. The archived manual geometry LOD writer passed offline format checks; EDF6 runtime-switching qualification remained separate.

Three different questions

  1. Can the material be loaded? Check the native shader/material ABI and required textures.
  2. Is this a valid lower-detail mesh? Check its geometry, UV and material identity and the applicable LOD association.
  3. Will EDF6 switch it in the intended situation? Requires a game/runtime observation; it is not proven by parsing the MDB or noticing a shader with LOD in its name.

Specialized FarMap shaders are worth preserving when found, but are not a prerequisite for every reduced-geometry model. Their existence does not establish a universal shader conversion recipe.

See also reading and writing native MDB materials and EDF5→EDF6 MDB migration findings.

Evidence boundary

The figures above come from a finite four-map native RAB census retained in our October 2026 research. They support the negative conclusion that special LOD shaders are not mandatory for every native geometry LOD in that corpus. They do not prove exact runtime switching thresholds, all model families, or a universal FarMap authoring algorithm.
