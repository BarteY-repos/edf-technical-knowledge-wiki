EDF5 → EDF6 Humanoid standing/stoop capsule contacts and welding

ConversionEngine RE & NetworkingGameplay & AIEDF 5EDF 6
Technical reference · Mixed: exact-binary static, native map geometry, reported modded-game runtime; causal contact hypothesis open

Scope and provenance. This page reconstructs a bounded case: EDF5 DLC1 DM001 imported into an EDF6 build using the native CAVE501 map and a stock EDF6 Humanoid_Basic-family enemy (E506, with E503/E617 controls). It connects measured map/character geometry with static binary analysis and reported modded-game runtime observations. It is not a universal rule for all enemies, map ceilings or retail builds. The underlying detailed research log predates the later practical route resolution; see the current DM001 case study.

The apparent paradox: why can the enemy traverse a gap it cannot initially occupy?

In the original diagnostic K spawn, the alien appeared at the specified position, was briefly visible in a pre-animation/T-pose state and then rapidly moved up through the cave ceiling. Similar behavior occurred for other Humanoid_Basic-family comparators, arguing against an E506-mesh-only defect. A delayed route assignment did not prevent the first-frame displacement: the early failure preceded execution of the scripted walking route.

The sampled native CAVE501 floor-to-ceiling clearance at K was approximately 15.7716 world units. Two nearby successful initialization positions, O and N, had approximately 15.9973 and 16.7999. Once initialized safely, a humanoid could later stoop and traverse a passage with approximately 13.8694 units of clearance. These figures do not establish a clearance threshold. Moving the spawn also changes which individual triangle, edge and contact manifold the capsule intersects.

At K, the vertical ceiling query hit near a triangle vertex: closest edge distance about 0.00481, ceiling normal approximately (0.0459, -0.9975, 0.0531). This near-vertical surface normal favors upward separation for a deep collision, but the exact solver response is not recovered solely from the geometry.

Shape dimensions and postures

Read-only reverse engineering reported the following nominal profile geometry in both EDF5 and EDF6 for the studied Humanoid_Basic class:

Profile Capsule spine endpoints, local units Radius Overall nominal vertical extent
Standing (D10) (0,4.5,0) to (0,20.5,0) 4.5 about 25
Low / stoop (D18) (0,4.5,0) to (0,7.5,0) 4.5 about 12

There are additional physical profiles. The same nominal standing geometry makes “EDF6 simply enlarged the alien capsule” an insufficient explanation for the observed difference. Initialization exposes the standing body; later locomotion can choose a smaller physical profile. The two phases must be evaluated separately.

Different Havok contact paths

EDF5 uses the older hkpCharacterRigidBody path. The audited code has character-specific capsule-contact unwelding: for eligible contacts along the standing capsule, a normal originating from local triangle geometry can be redirected using the capsule's axis/shape. Its depth eligibility boundary is near the negative capsule radius (-4.5 for this case). An ideal infinite roof plane intersecting the central capsule spine also yields a signed capsule-plane separation of about -R. That coincidence makes the actual edge/vertex feature and small changes of penetration depth important.

EDF6 uses an hknpCharacterRigidBody-based Sandlot wrapper. Its studied character-body quality is 10 (PRESET_CHARACTER), which requests flag 0x80 = ENABLE_NEIGHBOR_WELDING. The native CAVE501 static-body quality supports that bit. The character's configured capsule radius is passed into hknpCharacterRigidBodyCinfo.weldingTolerance (+0x6C in the audited layout), so its runtime material uses about 4.5 rather than the constructor's nominal approximately 0.1. This is an observed active parameter path, not merely a debug string.

The native CAVE501 world has enablePenetrationRecovery=1, collision tolerance approximately 0.05 and AABB margin approximately 0.02; the studied humanoid does not opt out of penetration recovery. The capsule also has a reported maxAllowedPenetration of about 1.484375. None of these values, individually, is established as a hard ejection threshold. In particular, knowing that neighbor welding is requested does not reveal the final welded contact normal or prove it alone caused the upward movement.

Engine-level distinction: EDF5's character-specific late normal rewrite and EDF6's earlier hknp neighbor-welded manifold handling are meaningfully different contact pipelines. The exact causal sequence feature selection → welded normal → solver/recovery output at K remains an unresolved reconstruction rather than a traced, runtime-verified chain.

Low-stance selection versus climbing

The audited EDF6 Humanoid_Basic state logic has a useful specific distinction. A condition equivalent to EAC & 0x100 or a nonzero 10F0 counter selects the low D18 profile. Two environment-query sites around virtual addresses 0x1804718DA and 0x180471C7B can set 10F0 = 16. While this counter remains active, the studied update bypasses a D28-related ClimbCollisionCheck branch and continues through the low-state path. These addresses are specific to the analyzed EDF6 binary; they are not portable patch offsets.

During earlier R33/R33C diagnostics, the actor could initialize from a high-clearance position but stalled while attempting to climb at the subsequent narrow passage. A source-like humanoid_climb_setting=[6.0,20.5] did not by itself resolve the initial eject or this later stalled traversal. That observation narrows the problem to stance, path and contact interplay; it does not prove that the low-state counter was the sole missing input.

Later accepted DM001 outcome takes precedence: the practical R33P-C repair kept a safe spawn and made a native lower-branch guide the first active route target, instead of first aiming at the problematic original WP0. The resulting route was accepted in the tested mission context, with an additional encounter-timing adjustment described in DM001 R33P-C. Do not present the earlier R33C stall as the current open mission blocker, or infer that the final solution changed EDF6's global collision engine.

What the evidence does and does not establish

  • Observed in the reported modded-game test: K first-frame upward displacement, successful alternative spawn initialization, subsequent low traversal controls, and the eventual practical R33P-C route result.
  • Static native research: matching nominal capsule profiles in the two generations; hkp-versus-hknp character contact architecture; EDF6 quality/welding settings, recovery flags and low/stoop state-selection sites.
  • Proposed mechanism, not demonstrated end-to-end: a K vertex-neighbor contact retaining an upward-looking welded normal and causing solver penetration recovery through the roof.
  • Not established: a universal minimum tunnel height, a per-map automatic route-repair algorithm, identical behavior in all EDF6 executable versions, or a general fix by changing only a single character SGO field.
