EDF4.1 UfoRobo in EDF6 — why an attacking boss can remain stationary

ConversionCross-game ConversionGameplay & AIEDF 4.1EDF 6
Technical reference · Source-inherited binary RE, pure state tests and historical observations; no fresh two-peer runtime

The important timer runs only in the correct AI state

A reported EDF4.1→EDF6 UfoRobo port continuously attacked but hardly moved. Inspection of the original EDF4.1 state machine and the retained bridge located the issue in the interaction of AI states A3/A4/A5 with body states S7/S8/S9, not simply a missing animation clip or bad terrain path.

  • S7: standing/choosing the next action.
  • S8: a step or turn animation, including root motion.
  • S9: an attacking animation.
  • A3: normal movement and countdown toward the next attack.
  • A4: wait until the body is ready to choose/start an attack.
  • A5: hold while the attack animation is playing.

In the old incorrect port, AI returned to A3 as soon as S9 started. Its attack timer could count down and queue another attack while the body still played the original long S9 clip. At the next S7, A4 immediately started another S9. Movement intervals were consumed inside the long attacking phase, yielding a stationary attacker rather than a walking enemy.

The preserved EDF4.1 logic stays in A5 while the body remains in S9 and goes back to A3 only after the body returns to S7. This keeps the attack timer from consuming walking time during a shot.

Historical traces identified S9 clips lasting roughly 28–37 seconds and attacks repeating in a way that kept the body in S9 across many 600-frame trace samples. These are historical test observations of that port; they are not a new measurement here.

The legs use multiple phases, not one walk target

The source Legs state stores phase-1 and phase-2 durations, target radius, optional sideways interval/duration, cruise direction and gentle turn rate. A studied randomization uses approximately:

phase1 = 1200 + rand(600) frames
phase2 = 600 + rand(300) frames
cruiseSpeed = ±speed (backward probability depends on approach state)
turnDuration = rand(300) frames
turnRate in roughly [-0.0075, +0.0075]

During approach, the actor moves forward or back according to horizontal distance versus its chosen radius; outside approach it may rotate toward its target before cruising. Sideways movement is a separate overlay. The body consumes discrete foot phases and clip root motion, not a continuous “walk forward” request.

At S7 the animation chooser depends on current foot position, whether a backward sequence is chained, target turn magnitude and pending attack. A foot already ahead must be returned or continued before an unrelated side-step can safely begin. Root motion is sampled from the source clip's X/Z/yaw keys and reconciled with the body's current transform.

Death is a monotonic state machine

The same bridge includes a separate fix for a remote copy that has already died. Death should be terminal, except for the progression:

living S0..S9 -> dying S10 -> wreck S11 -> deleted

Once dead, a host/network body event must not drag the guest copy back to S7, S8 or S9. Once in S10, only S11 is allowed; S11 may not transition back to a living state. Without this gate, the old failure could leave a destroyed robot as a lingering non-deleting “ghost.”

These findings are about native AI clock and body-state ownership, not a universal fix for remote state replication, pathfinding or source/target collision. The source has pure state tests; no new two-peer runtime tests were performed for this article.
