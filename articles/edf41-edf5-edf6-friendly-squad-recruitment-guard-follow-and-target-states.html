EDF4.1/5/6 friendly NPCs: recruitment, squad trees and combat states

ConversionGame technical referencesAI and navigationEDF 4.1EDF 5EDF 6
Technical reference · Attribution and grade per source fact; this edition adds no game runtime test

The squad system in EDF4.1, EDF5 and EDF6 is implemented as parent/child links between game objects, not as an independent roster manager with an autonomous “squad entity.” A friendly soldier tracks a leader and a list of followers. Its animation/combat AI chooses among guard, follow and engage states.

State-machine outline

Game Guard / Follow / Engage slots Update entry
EDF4.1 57 / 58 / 59 SoldierBase per-frame update, with direct move-and-look
EDF5 74 / 75 / 76 SoldierBase state machine with path controller
EDF6 89 / 90 / 91 Soldier Think with navigation and ChaseLeader distinctions

The common transition pattern is: a leader causes guard → follow, a valid hostile target can cause follow → engage, losing that target returns to following, and losing the leader returns to guard. A script may separately set route or aggression flags, and combat events can interrupt the visible behavior.

Recruitment is proximity plus eligibility

The examined EDF4.1 and EDF5 recruitment loops scan the friendly player's team for the nearest living HumanBase within 10 m; they skip remote player copies in online mode. The check is gated by an object-side “may join” field and by the fact that the soldier currently has no leader. It then links the soldier to the player and emits a join event/talk cue (合流). In EDF6 a corresponding +0x540 flag and the SoldierBase::Think recruitment block were identified.

This explains why simply spawning a Ranger or Wing Diver in a mission is not sufficient to guarantee that approaching the character recruits it. The mission source must establish eligible team/flag/leader state as well as spawn an appropriate soldier object. A modded mission missing one of those conditions is a content problem until native runtime evidence proves otherwise.

Leader death and squad continuity

In the examined EDF5 and EDF6 paths, a dead leader causes followers to unlink. An eligible soldier may subsequently recruit to the player, but the code does not demonstrate a universal automatic election of a new AI leader. EDF6's auto-revive-related flags can affect which branch applies. Online follow changes are messages handled through the host/remote distinction rather than only local animation updates.

EDF5 additionally contains a temporary cover/recruitment budget for some unrecruited mission squads. The source records a roughly 1800-frame (30-second) initial state with different detection/damage/weapon factors while still in an unrecruited AI root. Because its gameplay meanings partly rely on the EDF6-homolog interpretation, those precise factors should be treated as a source-backed cross-version inference, not an independently reproduced EDF5 combat test.

What these facts do not settle

  • They do not prove successful movement on EDF4.1 geometry imported into EDF6. Physics collision, nav graph and AI steering remain separate.
  • They do not imply NPCs obey a player-facing “stay” order. EDF6 has SetAiChaseLeader(bool) in scripts, but that is not automatically a manual player command.
  • They do not guarantee every friendly type is recruitable; team, flag, mission and enemy-squad configuration can differ.

EDF4.1 mission-native recruitment commands and boundaries

In the examined EDF4.1 mission-native surface the object-creation calls and the direct follower commands are different operations:

Native ID Name in the retained source What is asserted in the script contract
1010 CreateFriend Spawn friend, retain the supplied canRecruit state
1011 CreateFriendSquad Spawn a scripted squad, retain canRecruit
1012 CreateFriendGroup Spawn a group, retain canRecruit
3151 SetAiAutoFollow Explicitly arrange automatic following
3152 RecruitObject Explicitly attach a follower/leader relation, mapped through SetAiFollow in the audited conversion

A successful CreateFriend is therefore not equivalent to executing RecruitObject, and neither guarantees that the independent 10 m proximity recruiter is eligible to run. The source state machine, original leader, recruitment flag and mission commands determine whether the soldier joins the player. In the historical EDF4.1→EDF6 conversion, the five native contracts were checked statically; no post-R18 in-game recruitment PASS appears in that lineage. The audit is evidence of command identity/preservation, not proof of gameplay outcome.

Another source boundary concerns animation: the EDF4.1 friendly soldier does not automatically have the EDF6 ledge-climb states merely because its actor is hosted by the newer Soldier class. That is a cross-generation asset/state availability difference, distinct from the movement and navigation problems.
