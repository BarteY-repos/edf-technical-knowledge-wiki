EDF6 vehicle optics — physical sight anchors, FOV, zeroing and camera masks

EDF 6Engine RE & NetworkingGameplay & AI
Technical reference · Studied native camera behavior and optional custom optic design; no new runtime

Scope. This page records the original game's vehicle-camera mechanism, not instructions for implementing custom sights, first-person view, sight magnification steps or a ballistic-range drum. Those were design specifications for a separate adapter and are not established as stock EDF6 features.

The native ride camera is bound to the seat and model

In the examined EDF6 build, the ride camera's eye and look target can be read from model-authored MAB locators referenced by the seat's vehicle_riding_position SGO record. The seat carries parent-bone association, easing, a camera type and XAngleAdjust. When the animated parent bone moves, the camera's desired eye may move with it. This can place the eye behind the chassis, as observed for gun-attached locators, even though the character remains correctly seated.

The main native camera reference lists exact field offsets and examples from Blacker, Titan, Naegling, Nix and Proteus. See EDF4.1/EDF5/EDF6 boarding records for seat eligibility; seat eligibility alone does not define camera placement.

Zoom and camera clipping are separate native mechanisms

The player camera's FOV is derived from weapon/character zoom state in normal follow mode; it is not proven that a vehicle gunner automatically has the same scope input path. The inspected ride camera has its own state and locators. The native camera collision helper can constrain the eye with Havok queries, so magnification, obstruction and true gun line-of-fire must not be conflated.

In particular, source data containing SecondaryFire_Type=1 on a vehicle weapon is not sufficient proof that the shipped vehicle UI provides a working optical zoom mode. Nor do the historically proposed 1×/3×/6× magnification sequence, a round eyepiece mask, or configurable ballistic zero belong to the established list of stock EDF6 features.

Ballistic sight models are not native game measurements

A conventional idealized ballistic range model may use alpha = asin(g*d/v²)/2, with v and g expressed in compatible units. This is a generic model, not a measured EDF6 trajectory for every weapon class. EDF6 contains guided and class-specific projectile paths, and the gun's parent bone, aim controller and projectile origin can differ from the camera location.

Sources: EDF6 Camera Knowledge Hub, native camera field guide, and the independently analyzed vehicle seat reference. Historical custom optic design text is preserved outside the reader wiki in the R118 maintainer audit. No new game runtime test.
