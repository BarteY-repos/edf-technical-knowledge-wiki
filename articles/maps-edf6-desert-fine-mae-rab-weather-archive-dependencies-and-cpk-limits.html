EDF6 weather: Desert508 FINE MAE/RAB and CPK resource identity

ConversionMaps & NavigationWeather & resource bindingEDF 5EDF 6
Source-backed research / case study · Port-source and real-host CPK negative audit; bounded Desert Fine identity

Evidence: static reconstruction of a specific EDF5-source-derived Desert508 port running in EDF6, including a historically runtime-qualified byte identity for its Fine resources. A later real-host search of the scanned vanilla EDF6 CPK set found no qualifying DDS resources at the expected paths. This is not evidence that all native EDF6 maps or installations use this particular Fine profile.

Two independent resource carriers

In the studied compatible Desert508 Fine asset pair, an .MAE and an .RAB have different roles:

Asset Container Recovered contents/role Measured size
IG_DESERT508_FINE.MAE MARC weathers.dsgo weather profile plus the local ig_desert508.rmpa area/spawn record 7,344 bytes
IG_DESERT508_FINE.RAB SSA/RAB Four texture records with preserved type/index and compression metadata 48,676,035 bytes

The selected weather MAE represented 17 local weather shapes and 17 spawn records inside the RMPA payload. The weathers.dsgo block carries the reconstructed weather scalar/vector values. An MAE is therefore not interchangeable with a texture RAB: both supply different parts of the runtime environment graph.

Four named Fine texture dependencies

The examined RAB content depends on the following named resources:

Source resource identity What was verified in the bounded archive evidence
localenv_base_fine.lod.dds Exact typed RAB entry and compression identity
cube_sky_sun01_bc7.dds Exact DDS/CMPL representation
sun02.dds Exact DDS/CMPL representation
localenv_base_fine.dds Exact DDS/CMPL representation

For this source-derived pair, archive identity depended not only on the DDS pixels but also on the original SSA type/index, HD flag and ft1/ft2/z entry metadata. A raw DDS and a historically exact CMPL entry were alternative evidence inputs; byte-exact recompression/entry reconstruction was required for a matching final RAB. This does not establish a general rule that every game-side RAB must be reconstructed from these four names.

Resource location: do not assume the whole Fine pair is stored in the CPK

Initial resource searches assumed that the exact fine.mae/fine.rab or their four DDS members could be resolved from the selected vanilla EDF6 CPK archives. A later real-Windows-host inspection contradicted that assumption: the four runtime-qualified Fine DDS resources were absent from the CPK set scanned in that environment.

The historically effective source of bytes was an explicitly supplied local Fine RAB whose complete size and SHA-256 matched the qualified Desert port. This supports two narrowly scoped conclusions:

  1. A map's runtime-requested resource path and its discoverability as a standalone CPK entry are different questions.
  2. A working port may rely on separately preserved weather assets even when an inspected vanilla archive does not expose the same entries.

Do not extrapolate the negative search to every edition of EDF6, every CPK or the native rendering rules for unrelated maps.

What this does not establish

  • The source corpus does not prove that all native EDF6 FINE profiles have four DDS members or this exact RMPA grammar.
  • A matching RAB/MAE hash is not independent visual fidelity proof for a new authored map.
  • The real-host CPK negative result does not show that EDF6 lacks normal Fine weather; it establishes only an absent exact resource identity in the archives scanned.
  • The FINE variant is not among the two mission presets actually consumed by the retained Desert508 EDF5 DLC mission BVMs: those were EVENING and SUNSET, both separately runtime-positive in the port.

For the runtime-qualified consumer scope, see Desert508 source-weather closure. For actual _envMap name resolution, see MAE/DSGO environment binding.
