Engine packet transport versus per-object plugin authority

Packet delivery correctness does not by itself determine which machine is allowed to simulate an extra NPC driver or count one new projectile hit. Per-object multiplayer authority and local copies explains the 0x01/0x02 identity word, seat-0 owner fallback, registered versus unregistered copies and why a player's gunner input may be owned by a different machine from the vehicle.

Some of the most useful recovered engine facts concern which object owns a state, how much information is replicated, and what happens when a network link is replaced. Those rules are more informative than treating every multiplayer desync as a generic packet-loss problem.

Weapon fire direction and replicated projectile state

Observed code paths distinguish a per-shot fire transform from the ordinary actor orientation. Projectile direction cannot always be reconstructed from where a player avatar is facing. The game transports or reconstructs a subset of bullet creation and state properties according to subtype/selector-specific policies.

The retained native weapon/bullet findings separate direct-fire modes, star-dependent evaluation and NetworkBullet selector/contact cases. The amount and meaning of replicated information are profile-specific; a generic “send actor transform and replay” algorithm is not faithful across all weapons.

For exact selector/weapon family boundaries see weapon-fire direction, shipped weapon sync policy and NetworkBullet contact semantics.

Reliable state survives a transient physical-link replacement

The studied Sandlot RUDP/reconnect path separates the replaceable physical P2P Link from longer-lived packet Controller/UserSession/ReliableMgr state. A transient RemoteConnectionClosed can replace a physical link while pending reliable records, deduplication and sequence state survive on the logical peer/session layer. A true logical peer removal instead clears the UserSession and eventually destroys the retained state.

This explains why changing physical transport objects is not necessarily a full clean-session reset. Exact fault/retry timing and sticky error behavior must be interpreted from the appropriate engine and peer lifecycle, rather than assuming TCP-like semantics.

See RUDP and reconnect analysis. Third-party multiplayer patches can illustrate failure modes but are not proof of what original game binaries do.

What has not been proven

Recovered ownership and serializer shapes do not establish perfect eight-player multiplayer, desync-free behavior, or all runtime combinations. Network correctness depends on exact patch/version/peer behavior and gameplay-state constraints; offline inspection is evidence about implementation, not complete gameplay qualification.

For exact protocol identities and a diagnosis that separates what the native code proves from previously rejected theories, see EventController event IDs and cross-version desync investigation.
