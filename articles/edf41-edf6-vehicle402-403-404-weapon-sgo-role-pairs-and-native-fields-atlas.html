At a glance

What this page answers: The vehicle 402–404 atlas compares weapon files by both filename and mission role across EDF4.1 SGO and EDF6 DSGO formats.

How to read it: Check the 11 role-matched pairs separately from the 19 same-named files and five EDF6-only entries. Compare decoded fields, not byte offsets.

Evidence boundary: The sample is bounded to specific vehicles and assets. A same-name match does not prove the mounted weapon has the same behavior or owner.

Why same-name file comparison can mislead

This catalog decodes a bounded source asset sample for friendly vehicles 402 (Naegling-style rocket carrier), 403 (railgun tank) and 404 (heavy multi-weapon tank). The archived sample contains 19 original EDF 4.1 weapon SGO files and 24 EDF6 weapon DSGO files. There are 19 identical-filename pairs and five target-only file names in this sample. This is not a full inventory of all weapons in either game.

Original EDF 4.1 records in this sample use SGO version 0x102. The paired EDF6 entries use DSGO. Byte-by-byte diffs or direct re-use of offsets would confuse a format migration with a gameplay change; the tables below compare decoded named fields.

Crucial identity trap: the later role-aware R2 report compares original V_402ROCKET_ROCKETCANNON.SGO with EDF6 V_402ROCKET_ROCKETCANNON_AI.SGO for the mission AI launcher. Their AmmoCount is 100 versus 200. Comparing the same-named V_402ROCKET_AI_ROCKETCANNON.SGO on both sides instead yields 50 versus 50; it is a different file pairing, not a contradiction. Do not derive game AI loadouts from filename equality alone.

Even identical numeric fields do not establish identical runtime semantics when the class, physics, animation or event consumer differs. Ammo_CustomParameter, audio, animation and display paths are deliberately not counted as proven gameplay deltas. Source and target files can differ in many non-gameplay fields.
