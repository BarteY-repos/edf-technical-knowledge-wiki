EDF6 online difficulty caps: ModeList interpolation, red versus yellow levels, armor launch

EDF 6Game technical referencesOnline caps
Technical reference · Build-scoped static analysis; original contributor evidence grades cited; no new R126 gameplay runtime

EDF6 has a per-mode, per-difficulty online weapon limit, an armor-box limit, and a separate loot-level window. The equipment screen presents two visually different comparisons. A red weapon level means it exceeds the active online restriction; a yellow one can mean it sits outside an expected drop-level window. Yellow is not another way to display the online restriction, and the two thresholds do not always align.

Source of the caps

Each ModeList difficulty entry contains indexed weapon- and armor-knot lists. The native getters interpolate between adjacent values using the selected mission's normalized position t (clamped to 0–1):

  1. Let x = (number_of_knots − 1) × t; let i = floor(x).
  2. For an interior segment, interpolate between knot i and knot i+1 if both are nonnegative; a negative knot denotes no applicable cap.
  3. Weapon output converts the interpolated unit through trunc(value × 0.25 × 100) to the displayed weapon-level ceiling. Armor output is an integer box count, not final HP.

The main campaign's Inferno weapon curve, for example, is the source sequence [2.4, 3.2, 3.999, 3.999]; the DLC1 and DLC2 Inferno curves use [-1, -1] (no positive cap). These are values observed in the cited CONFIG.SGO corpus, not newly measured in game.

What red and yellow actually mean

UI indicator Native basis Consequence
Red level Current online cap predicate and room flag Marks an above-cap weapon; menu selection is still possible
Yellow level Loot/drop-window range from other ModeList fields Can appear even offline; not an online-cap refusal
Normal color Neither visual predicate applies Does not by itself prove a weapon will drop or pass every room rule

The red predicate is installed on the weapon-selection screen only for online mode and compares the cell's level to the computed cap. The confirm handler ignores the over-cap bit 0 and refuses the separate disabled bits 1/2. Thus the highlighted red entry can remain selectable; the launch/runtime constraints are separate.

As a concrete illustration from the static corpus, the original campaign Hard range has a yellow boundary around 3.75 at its beginning while the online cap is 5. The exact placement depends on progress through the difficulty's knot curve. These values should not be extrapolated as a universal fixed maximum for every mission.

Armor is processed when the mission starts

Session room switches for weapons and armor are distinct. The live cap-state flag resides at GameState+0x14FC8 in the examined build. Its update path combines the room's weapon-limit switch with whether the computed limit is positive. When a mission starts, the departure serializer transmits the equipped weapon IDs as they stand, but clamps the armor-box count if the current armor cap is active. The spawn path converts armor count into class HP using the native armor formula.

The examined native offline-mode entries do not carry a positive online limit. An external offline-limit modification that compares a cap in boxes directly to HP would conflate units; its behavior cannot be described as stock EDF6.
