EDF6 map versus mission data: placement identities, RMPA and streaming boundaries

UniversalMaps & NavigationMapsEDF 5EDF 6
Source-backed research / case study · Reconciled

EDF6 map geometry, placements, collision/nav and mission-local routes/triggers are distinct resource concerns. Labels used in content-production workflows do not establish native engine file types.

Map assets are not their individual placements

A native map asset/resource may be placed repeatedly. MAPB/MAPO placement identity, runtime model identity and mission RMPA shape names must not be conflated. Shipping MapObjectDestroy* and MapObjectInvincible mission helpers target named mission RMPA shapes, not a proven persistent game-wide placement ID; see shape targeting.

Map-side and mission-side RMPA are separate

A map's spatial bounds and move-limit records are not the same format contract as a mission's routes, spawn groups, shapes and event targets. Similar coordinate data does not justify a universal serializer or a shared index rule. In particular, the audited mission RMPA writer numbers mission waypoint IDs from zero; a MAPB-dependent indexing shortcut cannot be transferred to it.

Streaming/portal suspicion versus physical wall collision

One EDFROOM / DM019 runtime test removed a suspected high-only cross-wall streaming stitch. The AI targeting symptom persisted, while web projectiles still physically collided with the wall. This is negative evidence against that particular streaming relation as the sole cause. AI perception/line-of-sight, query filters and navigation consumers remain separate possible explanations.

The evidence does not establish a universal EDF6 road or portal schema. Inspect exact MAC/MAPB/MAPO/FMB/NAV family records and paired runtime effects before assigning a native role.

Open limits

Visual navigation components, physical collision bodies, streaming relations and mission routes may disagree. Structural validation of any one subsystem does not prove correct in-game AI behaviour in another.
