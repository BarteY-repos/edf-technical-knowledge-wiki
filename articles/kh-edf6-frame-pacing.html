EDF 6 — Frame Pacing (community source notes)

EDF 6Community Knowledge HubPerformance
Source-backed research / case study · Contributed research; per-claim static, verified, unverified; not independently re-tested

This is a source-attributed research appendix, not a chronology or a replacement for topic reference pages. The observations were contributed in the EDF Knowledge Hub export, snapshot 2026-11-14, revision 54f89cd, compiled by novae (ajane).

Read each statement with its evidence grade and exact build. Verified means a reported game test, static means reverse engineering or source inspection without a reported game test, and unverified is a hypothesis. None of these observations was newly tested for this wiki release.

Facts

Game loop period counter and wait sequence

Evidence: static · Build: steam-678ccb46 · Fact ID: edf6-game-loop-steps

The game loop 11A2E90 asks the period counter 11DAE60 twice per loop (at 11A2F4F before the wait, at 11A2F6B inside the Sleep(1) loop); the counter returns n = whole periods since [2139D18] (period = QPC frequency × 100 / 5994, approximately 1/59.94 s, correcting the older 6000 denominator) and advances the base by n periods; the loop runs min(n, 3) update steps (lea ebp,[rsi+3] at 11A2EDA, cmp ebx,ebp / cmova ebx,ebp at 11A2F76 / 11A2F80; the count at [rsp+0xBC] goes to the scene's virtual +8 at 11A3006), so a late loop catches up by at most 3 steps and drops the rest. See the successor record edf6-game-loop-steps-2 and edf6-tick-clock-period-5994.

Basis: fn.py read; --xref 11DAE60 finds only these two calls; call bytes E8 0C 7F 03 00 8B D8 85 C0 75 1C at 11A2F4F, E8 F0 7E 03 00 8B D8 85 C0 74 EA at 11A2F6B

Addresses (build-specific RVAs unless indicated): 11A2E90, 11DAE60, 11A2F4F, 11A2F6B, 2139D18, 11A2EDA, 11A2F76, 11A2F80, 11A3006

Attribution: novae (ajane) · 2026-11-13

Source-record successor: edf6-game-loop-steps-2 (source, static) corrects the timing constant while preserving the same call sites and three-step catch-up cap.

Update loop counter and multi-step flag

Evidence: static · Build: steam-678ccb46 · Fact ID: edf6-game-loop-tail

When n > 1 the loop sets [rdi+0xC] = 8 (11A3079), counted down by one per loop, and passes [rdi+0xC] == 0 as a flag to 111D890 (meaning unread). The loop's end calls the getter 1183310 at 11A30BC and 1129C10 with 2139A90 (the render command queue) at 11A30DF.

Basis: fn.py read

Addresses (build-specific RVAs unless indicated): 11A3079, 111D890, 1183310, 11A30BC, 1129C10, 2139A90, 11A30DF

Attribution: novae (ajane) · 2026-11-13

One simulation update per Present at 60 FPS

Evidence: verified · Build: steam-678ccb46 · Fact ID: edf6-content-cadence-even

At a steady 60 FPS in a mission the loop runs exactly one update step per loop and one loop per Present, so each shown frame advances the game by one 1/60 s step (about 99.5 % of frames; the rest a 2-step frame next to a 0-step one, about one pair every 7 s), with the window hidden and vsync off or visible with the game's vsync; the catch-up (2-3 steps) fires only while the mission loads. So on a 165 Hz screen EDF6's uneven look comes from the display side (#edf6-present-mode-pacing) and from 60 Hz motion, not from its stepping; a refresh that is a multiple of 60 gives every frame the same screen time.

Basis: novae (ajane); which loop's state each Present shows is not measured

Addresses (build-specific RVAs unless indicated): 11A2F4F, 11A2F6B, 11DAE60

Attribution: novae (ajane) · 2026-11-13

Present call hook byte sequence

Evidence: verified · Build: steam-678ccb46 · Fact ID: edf6-present-hook-site

The game's one Present call can be hooked by a 25-byte jump at 1118B2F (48 8B 8E 90 00 00 00 48 8B 01 45 33 C0 66 0F 73 D8 08 66 0F 7E C2 FF 50 40): the swapchain is [rsi+0x90] (an IDXGISwapChain; back buffer DXGI format 28, R8G8B8A8_UNORM), movd edx,xmm0 at 1118B41 loads the SyncInterval (1 in a mission, 0 on the loading screen), call [rax+40h] at 1118B45; copying the back buffer there gives real pictures of the game.

Basis: novae (ajane)

Addresses (build-specific RVAs unless indicated): 1118B2F, 1118B41, 1118B45

Attribution: novae (ajane) · 2026-11-13

Inactive off-screen window reduced frame rate

Evidence: verified · Build: steam-678ccb46 · Fact ID: edf6-hidden-window-vsync

A game window kept off screen and never activated runs at about 47-51 FPS instead of 60 (about 12 frames a second take about 33 ms) because of Present's vsync wait; forcing SyncInterval 0 while hidden brings 59-60 FPS, the game's own limiter then holding the rate. Not the cause: the timer (Sleep(1) lasts 1.7-2.0 ms in the process, resolution 1.00 ms), the GPU (25-45 % busy), OBS.

Basis: novae (ajane); hidden with SyncInterval 0, 509-546 and 0-8; visible about 530 and 1

Addresses (build-specific RVAs unless indicated): 1118B41, 1118B45

Attribution: novae (ajane) · 2026-11-13

Borderless legacy blit-model presentation

Evidence: verified · Build: steam-678ccb46 · Fact ID: edf6-present-mode-pacing

In borderless mode EDF6 presents with the legacy blit model (PresentMon: "Composed: Copy with GPU GDI", SyncInterval 1, no tearing), so DWM composes it at the desktop's refresh and VRR (G-Sync / FreeSync) cannot engage; on a 165 Hz desktop its steady 60 FPS reaches the screen as an uneven 3-3-3-2 refresh pattern.

Basis: two 60 s PresentMon captures of novae (ajane)'s play (2560x1440, 165 Hz FreeSync screens, 3594 frames each): displayed 18.2 ms (3 refreshes) 2645 and 2644 frames, 12.1 ms (2) 914 and 917; |MsAnimationError| mean 2.59 ms

Attribution: novae (ajane) · 2026-11-13

Special K flip-model frame pacing observation

Evidence: verified · Build: steam-678ccb46 · Fact ID: edf6-special-k-pacing

Started through Special K (120 Hz, its own 60 FPS limiter, flip discard, tearing allowed) EDF6 presents as Hardware: Independent Flip with SyncInterval 0, but about 2-2.5 frames a second are held 25 ms or 8.3 ms (|MsAnimationError| p95 7.95 ms); with Special K's Latent Sync (each present timed to a scanline in the blanking) the displayed time has sd 0.96 ms and 0.13 hitches a second. Reading (unverified): a frame paced by a free CPU clock lands on either side of a refresh boundary as it jitters. NVIDIA's G-SYNC indicator showed on although the captures stayed on 8.33 ms steps (unresolved).

Basis: three PresentMon captures of novae (ajane)'s play, 2026-11-13 (two plain Special K runs matched; one with Latent Sync)

Attribution: novae (ajane) · 2026-11-13

59.94-Hz clock and fixed simulation steps

Evidence: the Knowledge Hub frame-tick-clock-and-per-frame-points.md, pinned to Steam build 678ccb46. Native disassembly statements are static; contributor runtime samples and third-party patch performance claims remain identified separately.

Tick period is 59.94 Hz: 11DAE60 computes whole elapsed periods from QPC time and boundary [2139D18], using period freq × 100 / 5994 (a multiply by 0xAEF006D5604455C3 and shift 12 implements the bounded integer division). It stores the last whole boundary after a positive tick count. This is about 16.683 ms per scheduled tick, whereas game clock System+0xCE4 advances 1/60 seconds per simulation tick; the approximately 0.1% distinction must not be silently conflated.

QPC state ownership: boot base [2139D10] is initialized through CRT 316B0 and later game setup 11A3796. Whole-period boundary [2139D18] changes only within 11DAE60; the audited timer call sites 11A2F4F and 11A2F6B are on the game thread. The next scheduled tick boundary can be expressed as base + last + period. That describes the studied timer path, not a guarantee against every external clock change.

Main loop: at 11A2ECA the frame first obtains n = min(11DAE60(), 3), and a real elapsed wall-frame dt from 66D00. An internal one-shot byte at [rdi+9] can force dt=0,n=0; its setter was not traced. The loop samples input 1182A10, passes {dt,n} to Application::Update 705530, performs the render fence/queue step 111CC20, and updates a small counter when multiple ticks were taken. Thus n=0 is a valid render-frame case, not automatically a frozen application.

System Update separation: 1198DA0 adds n/60 to system/shader time when unpaused, submits view/frustum work through 11978D0 → 118DFF0 before the fixed-step loop, and skips most simulation when n=0. Each executed tick increments step counter System+0xCE0, updates objects and advances the CharacterGhost camera whose live matrix is later used for world rendering. Loading/nonmission and paused paths have different subloops. Consequently an n=0 frame may present the prior simulated state, and rendering cannot be described as one full physics update per Present.

Why presentation-only unlock is safer · static design reasoning: weapon and bullet SGO values, AI/action timers, Havok step length and network peer cadence all depend on a fixed game tick. The contributor recommends interpolating presented camera/object poses while leaving those systems at the original tick rate; direct simulation frequency changes require separate semantic audits. The precise origin of the swap-chain Present(SyncInterval,0) interval used near 1118B45 was not traced by that source.

N=0 work, input edges and unlock hazards

Network/platform work always runs: Application::Update's per-frame block calls SteamAPI_RunCallbacks, checks foreground state and conditionally reports it to EOS, updates the global network-like object at [20B2AC8] and calls EOS_Platform_Tick via 12ADB30, regardless of n. The name Network::Update comes from a third party rather than a verified RTTI identity. Running extra presentation frames therefore changes callback frequency even while gameplay ticks remain at 59.94 Hz.

Per-frame timers and vegetation: input-suppression countdown Application+0x6C decreases once per loop, while another clock +0x68 accumulates dt. MapMgr-like call 1230A0 also runs once per frame, incrementing a phase +0xCC by constant +0xC8=0.0125 unless paused, with a huge-number wrap guard. A third-party foliage/wind hypothesis matches those arithmetic reads, but the phase's rendering consumers were not fully traced. Extra n=0 frames can therefore speed that phase and shorten a frame-based input lockout.

Other per-frame calls: 7058F7, 705946, 705957 and 7059BE send dt to various subsystem routines (including sound), and 7058B7 dispatches HUD/render work once per frame. Whether every recipient is truly frame-rate-independent was inferred from arguments, not confirmed by reading its full body. The contributor proposed a timer-driven replacement for game-loop Sleep at 11A2F65 and window-thread Sleep(0) at 11844EA; these are plugin patch ideas, not stock behavior.

Mouse and wheel sampling: input callback 1188270 consumes and clears scroll-wheel accumulator [21360C0] via 11832D0, and takes relative mouse movement accumulator 1FEEE20 via 11831F0. Source writes MouseMoveX/Y action values through 1182A00 by overwriting, not accumulating. Input callback dispatch every loop is highly likely from 1182A10, but was not proven through every vtable layer. Reading and clearing events more often than the simulation can consume them is a concrete possible data-loss boundary.

Press/release edges: action wrapper 1182BC0 first clears pressed, released and repeat-pulse bytes +0x11/+0x12/+0x13, then rebuilds them from that frame's changes. A new press starts a repeat counter at 16, decreases it by n, and subsequent pulses recur at 2. Since the simulation consumes action edges during the next System step, two successive render frames with n=0 can erase an edge before any simulation step sees it. The source reports an offline 200,000-frame randomized loss test; no fresh input run was performed here.

Two-site wait-bypass patch · static consequence: removing game-loop Sleep near 11A2F65 and the branch at 11A2F74 produces extra n=0 frames without speeding fixed steps. Per-frame wind phase, callback and network work run faster, while overwritten mouse samples, scroll impulses and action edges may be lost. These are consequences inferred from call ordering; the patch itself was not rerun or certified stable.

Window-thread pump and third-party 120-fps reports

Window input pump: in-game window step 1183880 drains Win32 messages, snapshots GetKeyboardState at 21360E0, performs foreground/window checks, accumulates locked cursor offset from center into 1FEEE20 and recenters it with SetCursorPos. Game-thread 11831F0 takes/clears that accumulator. The render-fence routine 111CC20 also pumps messages and yields, but its observed waits were mostly real event waits in the contributor's sample.

Busy yielding window thread: 1184460 loops window states and calls Win32 Sleep(0) at 11844EA until quit flag +0x29. This yields to runnable equal-priority threads but does not block for a message. In one 90-second M001 sample with plugins loaded, the contributor attributed 82 s CPU time to window thread (72% sampled stacks in Sleep(0)), 16 s game thread and 11 s render thread. The figures are one setup, not a universal EDF6 CPU profile.

120-fps research package · mostly unverified: the source quotes a third-party package that explored the frame timer [2139B90]+0x18, interpolation near 118DFF0, and Umbra visibility-rate experiments. Its own RTX 3060-laptop reports were ~59.92 simulation ticks/s, ~90 Presents/s and ~90 distinct camera matrices/s, with remaining 60-step actor/VFX jumps. It also reported black flashes or load failures from visibility shortcuts and approximate Umbra/fence costs; raw program assets and independent runtime reproduction were unavailable. Only a subset of timer setter/callsite details was statically rechecked, so this must not be treated as a validated 120-fps mod.

Game-loop timer, catch-up clamp and zero-step rendering

Source: six records in the separate EDF6 frame-pacing.md source group, complementing the prior fixed-tick-clock notes. The two sources overlap on the three-step bound; different fact IDs preserve distinct cited analyses without presenting them as separate mechanisms.

Independent performance audit of the clamp: the same native 11A2F76/11A2F80 sequence limits catch-up updates to three and discards older missed ticks, rather than preserving an unbounded simulation backlog. A loop that wakes ahead of its next fixed tick can still produce a presentation without another simulation step. This source record provides a second provenance for the same path, not a distinct type of accumulator.

Zero-step draw path: scene virtual +8 (705530) receives frame seconds plus update-step count and calls 1198DA0. The system increments simulation clock +0xCE4 by steps×(1/60), invokes renderer 11978D0 even with zero steps, but jumps from 1198FB3 to 1199734 past objects and camera update 11995A6. Removing the presentation limiter therefore repeats the old world pose/camera when steps are zero. The static path does not show smoother movement unless distinct render-side interpolation of both camera and actor poses is added.

Legacy set_fps command: EDF6 constructs an EDF5-style timer through 963D90 at loop-object +0x18; console handler A0020 still changes its rate +0x20 and period +0x30 through 963DF0. The audited 11A2E90 loop never reads fields past +0x14 and instead gates on 11DAE60 and Sleep(1). Thus changing set_fps likely does not alter this loop's pacing, but other timer readers were not globally enumerated and no console experiment was run.

Graphics packet producers, fence ownership and measured visibility cost

Graphics work taxonomy: 11B6AA0 emits main world/type-0 visibility packets; auxiliary 11B6F90 emits type-2 packets (two persistent 0x150-stride entries, 0x2A0 total, with noted mask 0x40000000). Worker callbacks include heavy renderer snapshot 11B5A70 (60 payload records plus header, reaching Umbra::Camera::processVisibility), 11B5B30 (61 plus header), deferred release 1120B90 (one payload), state/resource bind/update 1127000 (seven payloads) and related 1126080/11262F0. Packet counts alone cannot estimate CPU/GPU execution cost.

Ownership at the fence: main loop 11A305A synchronously waits for xgl::gs::Device completion through 111CC20, then 11A305F calls 119FB40 to release list-owned nodes and objects. Moving that wait without extending resource lifetimes risks use-after-release; a deferred worker must capture the correct presentation generation, rather than reading later global simulation tick counts. In the contributor's one-scene test, lowering type-2 frequency caused black-surface flicker and laggy coarse shadows, while skipping a required native resolve stalled map loading. These are warning observations, not a recommendation to omit synchronization.

Single-machine D3D11 measurements: in a 26-window, ~130-second RTX 3060 Laptop sample, weighted Present was 59.318 Hz, first graphics fence 4.240 ms, callback 11B5A70 3.169 ms/call and the other sampled callback 0.0562 ms; Umbra::Camera::processVisibility dominated the two callbacks (reported 99.856%, with 3.36% of its wall time in Wait/GetData). Disabling Umbra occlusion worsened rather than improved the sample: 57.313 Hz, 6.584-ms fence and 3.288-ms visibility callback against the compared 2.387-ms baseline. In a separate 20-window ~91.294-Present/s trace, 3,149 zero-fixed-step frames had 9.107-ms fence waits and 5,992 step frames 5.452 ms (overall 6.711 ms). The source cautions against reading this as extra rendering work caused by zero-step frames; one machine and scene cannot generalize performance.
