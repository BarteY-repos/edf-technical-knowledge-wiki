EDF6 EOS V/A/B/C two-PC matrix: packet delivery vs Soldier state

EDF 6Engine RE & NetworkingNetworkingEDF 5
Technical reference · Eight real two-PC traces: bounded positive packet-path evidence; fix efficacy and 0x3500 unproven

Evidence: Direct two-computer EDF6 runtime traces analyzed on 7 September 2026, eight primary logs across four variants and one exact EDF6 binary. These observations apply to the tested traffic, session and build; they do not prove universal packet-loss freedom, correct remote animation, or a successful fix to player desync.

What the four variants actually changed

The baseline V left the transport unchanged. A enabled bAllowDelayedDelivery=true for the audited ordinary unreliable EOS physical send, while B provided only the Link READY reset and C combined the two. All four variants had the same canonical EDF.dll SHA-256 0d5092393a1b9d8f1b75b0c93d8a8f65615a36a92c46e40349c2b66c733d0916 and the same trace-DLL identity. The trace explicitly proved A/C activated the intended physical-send option; this was not a silent failure to load the test change.

The recipient's RX3300_PARSE count also equalled RX3300_ENQUEUE in every direction. Every observed EOS physical SEND returned Success: zero observed NoConnection, LimitExceeded or other nonzero send results. The dominant remote Soldier applied its NetworkObject updates at an approximately 10 Hz cadence in all variants, without a measured gap greater than 125 ms over each corresponding host interval. These are distinct checks: network delivery, SceneObject parser admission and concrete apply cadence.

The exact direction-by-direction figures are in data/R158_EDF6_EOS_VABC_3300_TWO_PC_RUNTIME_MATRIX.csv. They show that ordinary EOS packet disappearance was not the failure in this capture. They do not establish that decoded position changes reached the final world/render/physics transform correctly.

Why A, B and C do not establish a repaired desync

The A/C delayed-delivery bit changed as intended, but the baseline already delivered all recorded ordinary SceneObject messages. There was therefore no observed physical loss for A to correct. The B READY-reset branch was not meaningfully exercised: neither mid-session stale READY nor a matching reconnect failure signature occurred. Normal READY=0 INITIAL transitions and late teardown-related clears are not evidence of that bug. The combined C run inherits those limits.

DAMAGE_EVENT totals matched across peers per run, but 0x3500 NetworkBullet was not exercised (BULLET_TX=0 and RX3500_BULLET=0). Do not claim a projectile synchronization result from an ordinary movement matrix.

What the later two-PC capture added

The subsequent narrower R7 capture and R8 investigation found a different, later boundary: persistent position divergence (approximately 183 world units in the reported case) while sender updates continued and remote position-bearing staging/consumption became sparse. Matching physical packet hashes did not guarantee matching actual Soldier transforms. The later R8/R9 work therefore targeted per-object SceneObject staging, sequence freshness and concrete Soldier application rather than blindly changing EOS reliability. Do not conflate the R6 matrix's stable apply call cadence with proof that every individual call applied a fresh positional payload.

A received absolute transform should generally recover after a single lost snapshot. Persistent divergence with present physical packets instead demands checks for packet-field composition, generation/index/type keys, decode/stage freshness, overwritten pending state, player movement controllers and world-transform interpolation. An action/jump 0x3400 or strong correction path must be analyzed separately from ordinary 0x3300.

Timing and authority limits

The PCs had substantial and varying Windows wall-clock offsets. Ordered cumulative captures suggest a low-latency local path, but one-way per-record latency is not established from unsynchronized clocks. Host-local apply intervals avoid that cross-PC ambiguity. Also, the observed results do not invalidate the separate stale-READY reconnect hypothesis: that failure state simply did not occur in the V/A/B/C sessions.
