EDF5 scenery destruction: durability, hit flags, structure cascade and tree falls

EDF 5Game technical referencesScenery and destruction
Technical reference · Source static evidence with unverified behavior labelled; no new in-game R132 test

EDF5 has a MapObject_Base path with stored durability, child destruction and network messages. Separately, generated trees are handled by MapObject_Trees / MapObject_Tree handlers with no ordinary HP threshold. MapObject_CrashModel also has a mesh replacement function; the existence of that method does not prove it is a physics tick.

A MapObject_Base hit

In the studied executable the base object stores maximum and current durability at +0x124 and +0x128, respectively, with break state around +0x120. A hit record includes damage at +0x50 and a flag word at +0x5C (these are EDF5-specific offsets). Certain flags or definition categories allow damage to reduce durability, clamped to [0,max]; collapse can also be forced or triggered when durability reaches zero.

The object provides a no-write hit check, a hit-application callback, a collapse callback and a network-message path. An object can refuse a hit due to its current state, category or the embedded NetworkObject. The collision code does not collapse every decorative object on every projectile contact.

Definition categories and observed behavior

A 24-map tally included categories for buildings, trees, ground, poles, fences, vehicles/props, water and rocks, with different durability values. Buildings could have values in the hundreds; light poles, fences and some small props commonly had very small values. In the native hit path category-specific masks control whether a normal hit counts, and explosion/break flags add another gate. A 0/1 distinction cannot be reduced to the model's visual size.

Collapse is a family of effects

On collapse, the loader can traverse a MAPB child-index list and signal child objects before the parent; MapObject_Structure overrides the callback to step through its parts. MapObject_CrashModel has a mesh-swap override which loads a replacement resource while holding a Win32 critical section. Its fixed counterpart does not add a distinct override. An effects routine resolves numbered resources near the destruction point, but the source did not prove which spawned debris class owns that visual.

ModelDebris has an independent elapsed-time field (at +0x330) and cleanup threshold. That shows a timed lifetime; it does not establish that the class is the entire building-sinking mechanism, and the source marks its interpretation as unverified.

Why generated trees are different

A generated tree handler resolves the contact body's identity through a map of registered trees. On a qualifying contact it can call the tree-fall handler without testing HP. The tree's fall geometry is constructed at its base, and its own body/pose path is used. The .trb rule's [1000] number is stored as a Havok property and is not a normal health bar.

Evidence: build steam-5d11de53, reverse-engineered damage dispatch and class vtables; no new in-game test. Class names and indices should not be reinterpreted as identical EDF6 hknp behavior.
