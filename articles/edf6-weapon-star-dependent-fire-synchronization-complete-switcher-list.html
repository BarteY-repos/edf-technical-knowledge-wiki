EDF6 weapon stars and networking: 15 subtype1/direct switchers

EDF 6Engine RE & NetworkingWeapon stars and projectile synchronization
Technical reference · Source-derived EDF6 static binary/corpus evaluator, no new game-runtime parity

Key result: For the examined EDF6 weapon resource corpus, the same weapon may use a different initial multiplayer fire-creation policy at different reachable star states. A weapon's name, SGO file and nominal AmmoClass alone do not fix its network behavior.

Static census of the studied shipping configuration corpus

The retained source scan covers 1,315 active WEAPONTABLE weapon configurations with non-empty AmmoClass. Correlated reachable star-state evaluation used the game's observed seven-element WSLRF records, including the rule that fields sharing a save slot use the same current star value. Results (configuration counts, not individual guns fired in runtime):

Evaluated policy envelope Configurations
Always direct, full sender transform (sync_level=1) 669
Always direct, sender orientation with local translation (sync_level=0) 162
Always direct, receiver-local transform (sync_level=-1) 230
Always subtype1, batched receiver-local construction 157
Star-dependent policy (any change among possible direct/subtype modes) 97
Total 1,315

Of the 97 star-dependent configurations, 15 can switch between subtype1 and a direct path. The remaining 82 vary among direct synchronization outcomes only. Exactly 172 configs can reach subtype1 at some star state (157 always + 15 sometimes).

Exact three-outcome example — Penetrator F

AWEAPON104.SGO exposes three reachable outcomes in the retained correlated star envelope:

Saved star slots (others 0) Native evaluated values of interest Initial fire policy
slot 0=0, 2=0, 4=0, 5=0 FireInterval=60, FireAccuracy≈0.021 Direct, sync_level=0
slot 0=0, 2=0, 4=0, 5=3 FireInterval=45, FireAccuracy≈0.021 Batched subtype1 / receiver-local
slot 0=0, 2=1, 4=0, 5=0 FireInterval=60, FireAccuracy≈0.0039732 Direct, sync_level=1

Thus improving rate of fire can move the weapon to subtype1 while improving accuracy can move it to full sender transform. These statements are derived from the recovered native policy/evaluator and shipping SGO configuration; their exact in-game synchronization at these star levels still requires a two-peer runtime test.

Other useful discriminators

  • Dunkel N202: star-dependent accuracy produces subtype1 or direct sync0; it is not enough to examine FireInterval.
  • Penetrator and Buster Shot: changing FireInterval from a slower sample to a faster sample can switch direct sync0 to batched subtype1.
  • Double Lance and Twin Lance: a reachable AmmoCount increase from 1 to 2 changes the evaluated policy from direct sync0 to subtype1.
  • Lightning Bow family: the sample FireInterval change from 3 to 2 produces the subtype1 alternative for seven named configurations.
  • Raijin and Raijin α: better sample FireAccuracy can change subtype1 to direct sync0 despite FireInterval=1 in both samples.

WSLRF evaluation versus weapon-stat display formatting

The native evaluator was identified around EDF+0xD7CE0. The seven-slot weapon-stat record describes base value at star 5, setting type, save slot, per-stat max star, factor, exponent and a metadata/display flag. The setting type's low two bits distinguish increasing (0) and decreasing (1) in the examined data. The star denominator is 5.0.

Where the evaluated result becomes an integer gameplay field (AmmoCount, ReloadTime, FireCount, FireInterval), the native quantization is nearest integer with ties away from zero, implemented through an appropriate ±0.5f adjustment before truncation. This is not equivalent to naïve truncation or banker's rounding. The workbook's seventh-record display-precision notes concern formatting and must not be used to override native gameplay integer quantization.

Across the studied records, WEAPONTABLE star metadata agreed with the raw max_star fields for all checked pointer fields. This is a corpus-specific structural observation, not proof that all custom weapons follow it.

Why subtype1 matters for synchronizing an actual projectile

The initial subtype1 path uses batched/deferred receiver-local projectile creation and does not transmit the same muzzle-transform payload as the direct subtype0 family. Some projectile internal state is not reconstructed merely because later NetworkBullet selector messages arrive reliably. For the exact distinction between initial weapon fire policy and later projectile trajectory/contact state, see shipped EDF6 fire policy and projectile RNG and batching.

Full reference dataset and evidence limits

An optional companion data/EDF6_WEAPON_STAR_POLICY_1315_CONFIGS.csv is included in the public ZIP for offline filtering by weapon name, SGO, AmmoClass and reachable outcomes. It is a static parsed game-configuration dataset; the standalone HTML is self-contained and does not require this CSV.

The source-derived analysis evaluates correlated star states and the recovered static native call path. It is not a 1,315-weapon in-game test, does not demonstrate that every state has been legitimately obtained, and does not guarantee identical behavior after DLC updates or mods. The evidence originates in the EDF6 Engine RE weapon star envelope (EDF6_ENGINE_RE_WEAPON_STAR_POLICY_ENVELOPE_V1_2026-09-28.json and matching CSV) and the R10 binary/shipping-policy checkpoint under the private research library. No fresh game runtime or binary disassembly was performed for this wiki update.

For non-switcher weapons and direct-only star-variable configurations, consult the complete 1,315-weapon policy atlas. It indexes every active source configuration in the qualified R10 sample; the 15 cases above remain the detailed star-state examples.
