EDF6 damage by body part: armor pieces, boss cores and humanoid shields

EDF 6Game technical referencesDamage and combat
Technical reference · Contributor static analysis / source-graded observations; no new game runtime in R124

A visible enemy can contain multiple independently simulated parts. This is crucial for interpreting damage numbers and for understanding why a bullet can collide with a model while the target's main HP does not decrease.

Multi-part bosses and source body identity

Native Deroy leg/core pieces, some Queens and armored humanoids are not always one game object with a decorative material change. Some boss components are separate game objects with their own HP and damage events. Other targets, including UfoCarrier508 and MovingFortress in the examined source, use a ragdoll body's identity to determine which exact weak component accepts a hit.

The GameDamageInfo contact vector includes physical body IDs. A boss may demand that at least one contact maps to a particular core or open hatch part. Without the correct body mapping, the visual appearance of a hit is insufficient to prove damage eligibility.

Armor on humanoids

The investigated humanoid classes share a part lookup through their +0x6C0 part manager. Each armor piece holds its own durability and state. The main damage function asks the target for a part damage multiplier (virtual slot byte offset 0x88); an intact armor piece returns 0 for body HP damage. That yields the native white 0 damage number on armored Colonists and Greys, rather than a missing hit event.

When an armor piece's durability reaches zero it can break and change the effective part multiplier. This is distinct from killing the parent humanoid. A special hit flag 0x1000 skips armor-piece durability loss in the researched path; if the closest part still supplies a rate of zero, the result is zero damage to both armor and body. The source reports a game observation of white zeroes for certain gas and electric-line attacks against armored Greys, but the mapping of that flag to every such projectile is not completely traced.

Carried shields use a separate receiver

A humanoid's shield (EDF6_SoftBodyWeapon_Shield) is a weapon-derived SceneObject, not a normal GameObjectBase. It receives the same hit-message family but handles it in its own shield routine, outside the parent's ordinary damage function. Its guard_settings include maximum/current durability, per-hit cap, recovery delay/speed and reload time. A hit's shield damage is bounded by the shield multiplier and maximum-damage setting; reflection mode can also gate durability consumption.

Consequently, a hit absorbed by an intact shield produces no normal HUD damage number in the source-reported vanilla gameplay, unlike an armored body-part hit that can produce a white zero. Shield breaking and regeneration are state transitions belonging to that shield object; they should not be represented as merely an armor texture switching off.

Boundaries

This describes static code plus source-declared observations on EDF6 steam-678ccb46. The exact 0x1000 hit-origin classification is not fully proved, and this article does not claim that all enemy shields or every boss part share one ABI. Build-specific field offsets are not safe generalizations to EDF5 or EDF4.1.
