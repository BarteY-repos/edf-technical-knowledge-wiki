EDF6 online combat: who applies hits, killing-blow messages and HP epoch sync

EDF 6Game technical referencesNetworking
Technical reference · Contributor static analysis / source-graded observations; no new game runtime in R124

EDF6 does not publish every nonlethal hit as a full record with attacker, contact points and event data to all clients. The investigated native path separates local hit processing, killing-blow events and HP-delta or absolute-HP synchronization. That distinction explains why two machines can temporarily disagree on a floating damage number or hit reaction while the target's health later converges.

Which machine owns a hit?

The ApplyDamage path first tests whether the current scene is online; then it considers attacker identity, a remote-copy flag, an optional forced-hit bit and a target-specific virtual decision. Ordinary game objects, controlled players and vehicles can answer that decision differently. A simulated bullet on a guest machine is not automatically authoritative merely because its collision system detects an intersection.

The source identifies a guest pre-filter that suppresses certain attackerless, non-forced hits when the room is active. This is not a rule that an attackerless hit never deals damage: the host's corresponding path may process it. Likewise, an attacker handle may refer to a vehicle rather than the player in its seat.

Network paths after local damage

Change Audited transmission principle
Nonfatal hit Accumulate health difference for a later delta message
Alive → dead transition Send the killing-hit event with a new target epoch
Periodic or forced reconciliation Send absolute HP with an epoch

For the investigated target object, an outgoing delta is an integer truncated from the accumulated local float difference. The sender monitors thresholds relative to maximum HP, time and dead state rather than broadcasting each fraction immediately. The receive routine compares epoch counters and discards stale events. A received same-epoch delta can update a living object without replaying the original weapon hit or its contact location.

In the source's implementation, the kill event is raised by the alive-to-dead flag transition. A later nonlethal HP delta with an older epoch can be discarded. This is one reason another machine may never visually reconstruct the exact sequence of hit numbers preceding a death. Periodic absolute HP messages can repair health totals but cannot recreate a missed stagger or projectile-internal state.

Why HUD observations can differ

The original contributor reported an online session where each player saw their own damage numbers. The local UI event is not the same as the network event. A shield-hit absence or armored-part zero is also controlled by the local target's native hit implementation; neither implies the ordinary position snapshot was lost.

Evidence and scope

These claims are sourced from static EDF6 RE plus that specifically reported online observation. They do not constitute a new paired packet capture or a proof that every desync is caused by HP epochs. The gameplay event packet layer, retained RUDP and projectile-state serialization remain separate mechanisms.
