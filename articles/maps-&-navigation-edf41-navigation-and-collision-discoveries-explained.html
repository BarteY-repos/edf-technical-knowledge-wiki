R19 correction on the 4,971-footprint study

Historical R19 only: a then-latest natural geometry/ranking path produced 5,026 active footprints (55 above the 4,971 historical oracle), and 4,948 belonged to a different intermediate trial. Subsequent R32 source-semantic reconstruction supersedes this count: group-union XZ ranking, raw cached-AABB max_xz >= 5.75, 4,994 pre-deduplication instances and 23 source-scoped FixB removals reproduce the required 4,971 population. The exact historical carve/HKT bytes remain unrecovered. The old R1/R1B harness did not preserve that threshold and is not current classifier authority. See how the body-eligibility/GroupGeometry distinction works.

The 7,872,840-byte figure occasionally cited in old research is a whole-MAC size; it was never MAPO size. The inner MAPO record-family relayout is described here.

Navigation across EDF generations is not just a set of waypoints that can be copied. The studied EDF4.1→EDF6 research combines map-object placements, collision-body identity, Havok-derived geometry, raster/triangle selection and game-specific navigation output. Its most valuable community finding is which parts can be reproduced exactly and which remain unknown.

Reconstructing navigable geometry

An exact reference from the studied four-tile map has 1,562 vertices and 2,928 triangles. A later historical target uses the same vertex count but 2,651 triangles, meaning 277 triangles were removed. Recovering the correct removal geometry is not the same as simply producing any triangulation with the right count.

The source geometry/placement analysis has recovered a 65-group selection with 1,405 placements, consisting of 251 zero-rotation, 1,153 yaw-only and one mixed-XZ placement. The 4,971 active footprint population has since been reproduced naturally under a source-semantic R32 candidate. The remaining blocker is the complete historical body eligibility/carve identity and the exact HKT bytes. A retained natural 277-face candidate is 381,544 bytes (+32) against the historical 381,512-byte LOW oracle; the historical +16 byte comparison came from a non-natural rank-swap control, not the best natural current candidate. Neither a matching face count nor NVMD identity qualifies an EDF6 runtime NAV.

What an implementer should take from this

  • Preserve native map-object-to-collision-body identity; not every geometric candidate is a valid binding.
  • Treat HKT/SHKT/MAD geometry, map RMPA and native NAV/NVMD as separate but interacting sources.
  • Distinguish measured source geometry from byte-exact target output and runtime pathfinding behavior.
  • Never “fix” a mismatch by arbitrarily deleting 277 triangles, adding/removing 16 bytes or selecting collision bodies to fit known totals.
  • Loading donor NAV from a later EDF can be a compatibility compromise rather than faithful reconstruction.

For exact structures and further details see native EDF4.1 navigation, MAPO and collision study, MAD/SHKT and map consumers explained.

Evidence boundary: exact source/target binary observations and offline reproductions are available for defined cases; the unresolved target-specific selection rules and full EDF6 gameplay/runtime equivalence must not be claimed solved.

Why later solid/capsule contact studies still do not finish the NAV conversion

On original SK_HEIGEN02 source mesh data, a 265-instance MAD/MAPB survey distinguishes 166 closed 2-manifold candidates from 99 open source surfaces. A radius-0.45 m, height-1.8 m illustrative capsule finds 243 closed-solid candidate 4 m cells plus 495 open-surface hint cells. The 158 source point-in-solid positive cells are a subset of the 243, but none of the candidate cells may be assumed impassable without collision-layer, agent-profile and game-runtime evidence. A tiny four-tile test changes 512 source terrain triangles to 504 by excluding four example cells while retaining three open-only hints; HKT structurally passes, gameplay does not.

Keep three separate questions: R1D HillyCity original-byte oracle, R31–R33 4 m original SHFB terrain tile-bake feasibility, and R34–R35 source-MAD 3D obstacle/contact hints on one SK map. None individually proves that the EDF6 Ranger/Fencer/Hector correctly navigates an EDF4.1 map. The latest source studies used a later-game HKT/NVMD backend; this is an output-format compatibility experiment, not proof of Sandlot's original EDF4.1 agent-size or navmesh-generation algorithm.
