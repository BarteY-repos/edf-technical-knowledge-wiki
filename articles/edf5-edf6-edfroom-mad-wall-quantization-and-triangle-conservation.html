Documentation / Conversion / Havok collision geometry and compressed mesh

EDFROOM MAD: native-compatible wall collision, quantization and triangle conservation

ConversionMaps & NavigationHavok collision geometry and compressed meshEDF 5EDF 6
Technical reference · Source-derived and exact bounded runtime evidence separated

A Havok compressed-mesh conversion may perturb the decoded coordinates of individual vertices while preserving shared seam continuity and physical blocking. The NG_DLC_EDFROOM03 research provides measured source-versus-target collision geometry and a later gameplay observation that distinguish those properties from AI decisions.

Full controlled collision inventory

The retained candidate MAD represented 101 direct compressed meshes. The source triangle census and the deterministic degenerate-triangle filtering result were:

Item Count
Source logical triangles before filtering 315,460
Removed by the bounded degeneracy predicate 17,509
Expected kept triangles 297,951
Target metadata triangle count 297,951
Independently decoded target logical triangles 297,951

The degenerate-filter study used a 1e-7 source-area tolerance. This is the converter's audited filtering rule, not proof that every native EDF6 map filters its geometry with this exact tolerance.

The candidate's compressed-mesh broadphase audit reported 166,084 expected shape keys, exactly 166,084 reachable unique keys, and zero missing keys, extra keys, duplicate leaf keys or >1e-4 bound violations in that examined run. The audited MAD also contained 289 physics bodies (287 static and 2 dynamic). Passing these checks establishes physical wire/graph consistency for this candidate; it is not equivalent to a full native Havok gameplay qualification.

Shared-section seams were preserved despite coordinate rounding

An independent exact identity audit examined 20 named wall/corridor/collider records, including multi-section wall geometry. Every audited record reported zero seams exceeding 1 mm, 5 mm or 10 mm, with maximum measured shared-boundary discontinuity 0 under the audit's source-key comparison.

This does not mean the target compressed positions equal source floating-point vertices exactly. The largest measured individual quantization errors included:

Collision record Triangles Maximum decoded coordinate error (source units) Shared seam maximum
ya_edfroom_roomb.roomb_collider 1,701 0.0458995 0
ya_edfroom_rooma.rooma_collider 966 0.0276907 0
ya_edfroom_corridor_slope.side_wall 144 0.0225413 0
ya_edfroom_corridor.side_wall 144 0.0217197 0
ya_edfroom_corridor_r.side_wall 164 0.0210661 0

Interpretation: the compressed coordinates are quantized; however, vertices that are supposed to represent the same seam remained co-located under the measured source-key identity relation. A nonzero source-to-target error is not by itself an opening in the wall. Conversely, this localized metric does not guarantee every triangle in every map is topologically or physically correct.

Independent in-game observation

In the affected EDFROOM/DM019 scene, characters could not walk through the tested wall and spider web projectiles hit the wall rather than penetrating it. Some spiders still aimed/fired toward allied NPCs on the far side. A later controlled high-only streaming-link change did not remove that AI behavior.

This makes the following explanation unsupported: “Spiders attack NPCs through the wall because the collision exporter created a gap.” The surviving candidate explanations belong to AI-vs-AI target selection, perception/LOS queries, agent-specific navigation and target routing; none was conclusively identified in the retained runtime experiment.
