Behavioral invariant vs binary identity

Both contributed static analyses describe a CharacterGhostCamera-family update that selects follow or ride mode and writes a final camera matrix. This similarity is useful for locating equivalent subsystems. It does not make an EDF5 address/offset/hook site valid in EDF6. The source crosswalk rates structural correspondence high but direct offset transfer low.

Camera modes and vehicle ownership

In EDF5 the camera target remains the soldier when riding; the vehicle is discovered through the ride link, not inferred from scene parentage. The contributed source marks this as verified in the named EDF5 build, while the EDF6 mapping is largely static. Do not universally transplant the EDF5 parent/link assumptions into EDF6 without confirming the owner chain and seat record.

In EDF6 ride mode the seat data participates in camera construction; an EDF5 hook that only adjusts follow-mode distance may have no effect in ride mode. The reported F7210 riding predicate has no direct callers in the contributor's binary reading; the corresponding condition is also present in a vtable method. Lack of a direct callsite alone does not prove that the predicate is globally unused.

Hook placement and visual stability

The EDF5 camera tail site 9F4F3 was contributor-reported as a working per-frame hook after matrix update. The EDF6 update and shake sequence must be located anew. A literal port of that patch can point into unrelated instructions, and only one installed patch may own a particular hook site without chaining/coordinated dispatch.

The EDF5 contributor also reported that the scene camera's projection could read invalid for whole frames during heavy close combat, with the cause undetermined; do not present a speculative render-thread race as demonstrated. See EDF5 guide and camera source fact.

Safe conversion / plugin workflow

  1. Match exact executable/build and re-identify the camera object, vtable and update routine.
  2. Compare member accesses instruction-by-instruction; do not add one constant to every EDF5 offset.
  3. Validate follow, ride, death/spectator, pause and camera shake individually.
  4. Distinguish a writable final view matrix from the authoritative input/look state that may overwrite it next frame.
  5. For first-person proposals, verify eye/head-bone source and weapon visibility/aim behavior rather than declaring first-person compatibility from a possible tail write.
