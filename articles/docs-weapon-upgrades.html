Weapon Upgrades (Stars)

EDF 6DocumentationGameplay & AIEDF 5
Technical reference · Community-provided Japanese memo; source-specific and not independently rerun

EDF 5 introduced a weapon upgrade/star system that the research memo reports as retained in EDF 6. Individual weapon statistics can level independently. A weapon's stars are data in three places, not merely a label in its description.

SGO upgrade record: seven numeric fields

A star-upgradeable field is represented by a ptr whose payload is a seven-number record.

Index Meaning Notes
0 Base statistic The contributor identifies this as the value at star level 5, not necessarily at maximum stars
1 Stat type Correlates with kind/direction of progression; numeric enum is not exhaustively decoded
2 Save/stat position index Must agree between weapon SGO, WEAPONTABLE, and WEAPONTEXT
3 Maximum star level Each statistic can have a different cap
4 Coefficient 1 (a) Controls deviation from the base value
5 Coefficient 2 (p) Exponent controlling progression curve
6 Display precision mode 0 integer/truncate; 1 one decimal and rounding, as reported for display formatting

Example: AWEAPON001 / Minion Buster uses separate records for AmmoCount, AmmoDamage, FireAccuracy, reload, range, velocity and firing rate. WEAPONTABLE stores the maximum stars in the matching position order. WEAPONTEXT uses placeholders such as $0 with an accompanying data record.

Progression formulas documented in the workbook

Let B be the base value, L the actual star level, a coefficient 1 and p coefficient 2. The memo uses 5 as the normalization level:

Increasing statistic:

V_inc(L) = B * (a * (L/5)^p - a + 1)

[ V(L)=B\left[a\left(\frac{L}{5}\right)^p-a+1\right] ]

Decreasing statistic (reload time is an example):

V_dec(L) = B * ((1+a) - a * (L/5)^p)

[ V(L)=B\left[(1+a)-a\left(\frac{L}{5}\right)^p\right] ]

At L=5 both formulas produce B. For example with B=100, a=0.8, p=1.5, the increasing curve starts at 20 for star 0 and gives 100 at star 5, while the decreasing curve starts at 180 for star 0 and reaches 100 at star 5.

Important: The workbook spreadsheet deliberately evaluates levels up to 10 and yields negative values for the decreasing curve at levels 9–10 with these example coefficients. This is a mathematical consequence of the formula, not proof that EDF allows a negative in-game reload time. Validate caps and coefficients before editing.

Editing checklist

  1. Choose the actual field and its type/position mapping; do not reorder records casually.
  2. Keep the same stat-index correspondence in weapon SGO, WEAPONTABLE and WEAPONTEXT.
  3. Preserve maximum stars and display formatting in each of the three locations.
  4. Validate increasing or decreasing direction using the game's target field, not just the enum guess.
  5. Avoid extrapolating beyond the configured maximum star level.
  6. Test a newly obtained item and an existing saved item; a static SGO round-trip cannot establish save compatibility.

Source: 星システム!B1:K38, 星システム!B42:L69, 星システム!B69:L235 and 星システム!B242:E278 in mod製作メモ.xlsx. Formulas and sample values are reproduced as reported by that source; no new EDF6 runtime verification was performed.

Native gameplay rounding differs from stat display formatting

The seventh element in the workbook's seven-number record is described as a display precision/formatting field. Separate EDF6 binary research identifies the gameplay integer field quantization after the common evaluator at EDF+0xD7CE0: nearest integer, half away from zero, rather than generic truncation. That behavior applies to examined integer consumers such as AmmoCount, ReloadTime, FireCount and FireInterval. Do not infer the engine's gameplay rounding from the workbook's display-text precision flag. See full star-dependent network fire-policy census and shipped weapon synchronization for the test scope.
