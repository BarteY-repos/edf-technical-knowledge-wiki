EDF6 native TeamManager — seven stock teams, relation matrix and custom-faction limits

EDF 6Engine RE & NetworkingGameplay & AI
Technical reference · Source-inherited exact-build native TeamManager RE; 16-team overlay is custom/unverified in current runtime

A team number is not just a colour or HUD label

The investigated EDF6 TeamManager stores both per-object team identity and a directional relation matrix describing how team a sees team b. The native manager in the cited binary uses seven rows, IDs 0–6, rather than dynamically discovering unlimited teams. Its constructor reserves each row, its relation vector and its all-teams iteration with fixed numeric assumptions.

The source research labels the seven native roles in the examined profile:

Team Studied engine role
0 Basic faction / unspecified player-facing side in the stock matrix
1 Enemy
2 EDF/friendly
3 Neutral
4 Enemy-of-1-only role
5 Empty vehicles / boarding availability
6 Mission environment

These numeric categories are specific native-team semantics from the studied game build. In particular, teams 5 and 6 are not spare numbers for arbitrary PvP factions: empty vehicles and environmental hazards/interaction paths rely on their stock relations.

The relation values

A native relation is encoded as one byte in a row, with values:

0 = neutral
1 = friendly
2 = enemy

The relation table has a team-to-itself friendly diagonal. In the examined stock matrix, enemy pairings include 0↔1, 1↔2, 1↔4; 0↔2 is friendly; other unequal rows not expressly configured stay neutral. A runtime mod should not assume relation symmetry as a universal law merely because the recorded stock relationships were largely symmetric: the implementation stores relation[a][b] and relation[b][a] separately.

An object's current team field and its saved “last team” field have separate lifecycles. In the studied object ABI, +0x314 holds current team, and +0x318 retains last team. Closing an actor with SetTeam(-1) preserves the last-team information. The value -1 is therefore a closing-state sentinel in the source research, not an available ninth or sixteenth faction ID.

Why extending teams needs more than one constant

The examined custom PvP adaptation expands capacity from 7 to 16, allocating additional faction IDs 7–15. That is a custom patch, not a native EDF6 capability. A legitimate extension must update every fixed-capacity consumer: row reserve, row creation/fill, nested relation-vector limits, and the all-teams enumeration bound. The source lists 15 separate exact-build patch sites for those allocations and loops. Merely changing one capacity field leaves native iteration and relation arrays inconsistent and can cause memory errors.

The game's mission-start and reload paths also restore stock relation matrices. The custom overlay is therefore re-applied after those resets, not just once at startup. The demonstrated policy keeps the stock rows/columns for teams 5 and 6, including their relationships with any new factions, and validates the diagonal and every relation byte.

Portability and risk

The table, row stride (0x38) and fixed-capacity sites were reverse engineered for a single documented EDF6.dll build. Do not patch another executable at those offsets, assume the game supports 16 factions natively, or claim host/client PvP correctness based on a source patch. A robust implementation must retain exact-build signature checks, preserve original gameplay relations, and qualify damage, AI targeting, vehicle entry and network team synchronization in the actual game.

Source: components/compatibility/src/team_native_logic.h from EDF4.1 R10 HF4 R9 source research, with documented source-side native-TeamManager analysis. No new executable/runtime test was performed while writing this article.
