Overview

EDF6 multiplayer contains several distinct synchronization paths: actor state, damage, projectile/fire events, scene/object changes, mission events and transport lifecycle. A stable transform stream does not imply that HP, damage ownership or shot reconstruction is correct.

Evidence boundaries

A mod-defined tag 12 per-object channel is not a stock EDF6 wire protocol. Source research has documented several candidate fixes to desync, but an offline PASS, successful handshake or one-player mission load is not a full second-peer qualification.

Enemy attacks and network payloads

Native enemy damage formulas and the actual bullet collision stages are distinct from whether a projectile's RNG history, contact record or damage event was transmitted to another peer. Source-level projectile counts and level curves are not proof of network synchronization.

Direct two-PC EOS V/A/B/C runtime discrimination

The eight-log EOS V/A/B/C matrix proves the audited ordinary 0x3300 physical send, enqueue and parse counts match on both peers in all four runs, with a roughly 10 Hz remote Soldier apply cadence. That narrows the transport-loss hypothesis; it does not prove remote positional values were correctly consumed. Later R7/R8 evidence records the remaining SceneObject staging/application boundary. The READY-reset branch was not exercised by a mid-session reconnect and projectile class 0x3500 was not tested in that matrix.
