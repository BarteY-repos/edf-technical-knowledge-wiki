EDF5 versus EDF6 walking controllers: support states, gravity and moving surfaces

ConversionGame technical referencesCharacter control and physicsEDF 5EDF 6
Technical reference · Static source and contributor checks, with build and open questions noted; no new R131 EDF runtime

The examined EDF5 and EDF6 character controllers share recognizable physics concepts: a desired horizontal velocity, a separate vertical/ballistic velocity, a support-normal query and a 60 Hz step. A key detail is that Havok can report velocity for the supporting surface even while the character's stock walking code chooses not to inherit that velocity.

EDF5's native walking step

In EDF5 build steam-5d11de53, xgs::havok::CharacterControl_Walk has its own RTTI/vtable and a shared step routine at EDF.dll+0x628B80. It is owned by several classes, including HumanBase, HumanoidBase, MonsterBase, AlienTrailer, Barga/Balam-like fortress robotics and the Nix/Begaruta walking controller. It makes an hkStepInfo of 1/60 s and reads checkSupport into an hkpSurfaceInfo.

hkpSurfaceInfo field Meaning
Supported state 0 unsupported, 1 sliding, 2 supported
Surface normal Direction used to project movement onto a slope
Surface velocity Velocity of the ground contact, available but not consumed by the stock walk step
Dynamic flag Describes a moving/dynamic support-body property

When supported, the controller projects desired movement onto the ground and adjusts ballistic motion. A launch flag protects jumps from being immediately treated as ordinary supported movement; when airborne, the vertical velocity changes under gravity. The stock controller combines these fields into one character-body linear-velocity update.

The moving-platform trap

The EDF5 walk function reads the support state and normal but not the reported surface velocity or dynamic-support flag. EDF6's studied controller likewise calculates an output from its own movement and external velocity slots rather than automatically carrying over the surface velocity from a moving support body. Thus simply standing on a moving actor need not transport a soldier at the platform's speed.

For EDF5, the Havok support query does populate the ground-velocity field. Its getGround route, however, is called with useDynamicBodyVelocities=0, which can limit which ground-body motions contribute. Neither source establishes correct movement-on-every-vehicle behavior: whether a particular collision layer allows standing on a body and how that body's motion is represented remain separate questions.

Cross-generation differences and limits

EDF6's controller field offsets differ (for example desired velocity appears at +0x20 in the retained EDF6 map versus +0x50 in EDF5). Directly reusing memory offsets or moving-platform assumptions across builds is unsafe. The EDF5 source also notes that mode changes for ragdoll parts are a separate physical system from the ordinary walking controller.

Evidence: static exact-build reverse engineering, not a new test involving a moving platform. Potential alterations to surface-velocity inheritance in the original source were unverified engineering hypotheses and are not presented as native game behavior.
