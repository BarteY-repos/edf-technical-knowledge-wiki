EDF6 cling controller: insects, wall attachment, gravity, launch immunity and robot users

EDF 6Game technical referencesCharacter motion
Technical reference · Build-scoped static analysis; original contributor evidence grades cited; no new R126 gameplay runtime

EDF6 uses a distinct Havok-linked xgs::havok::CharacterControl_Cling for ground-attached and wall-attached actors. Its users include the InsectBase family (ants, spiders, bees, Dango and small dragons), Nephila, Berserker-family walking enemies, and the player's Vehicle502_GroundRobo. The same actor may change to a flight-specific mode that bypasses ordinary cling velocity integration.

Native state model

Controller field Meaning in the studied build
+0x00 Mode; nonzero bypasses ordinary velocity update (for example, GiantBee flight)
+0x50 / +0x60 Attachment normal and smoothed normal
+0x70 Attached/grounded byte
+0x73 Contact recorded for current step
+0x80 Ballistic velocity (a vector, meters/second)
+0xA0 Gravity vector; default (0, −9.8, 0)
+0xB0 / +0xB4 Collision sphere radius and extra probe distance
+0xB8 Number of frames during which landing is suppressed

When attached, the controller clears its ballistic velocity and relies on the attachment/movement branch. When detached, the update pushes ballistic velocity (plus temporary contributions) into the body and integrates gravity. The original ant/controller initialization scales gravity for one insect path; a single universal gravity constant for all enemies is therefore unjustified.

Launch is a velocity assignment, not a force impulse

The observed Cling Launch native sets the attachment flag to zero, writes a supplied velocity and records a no-landing frame count. Its callers use different counts: 2 for a sampled GiantAnt blast-knockback path, 0 for a jump, and 4/8 on robot paths. A zero-count launch can immediately reattach on the next contact; a nonzero count temporarily suppresses that landing. The launch alone does not switch animation state.

When the controller is in nonzero flight mode, this launch path does not provide the same result: the update bypasses its velocity integration, leaving movement under the actor's separate flight routine. That helps distinguish the handling of a grounded insect from a flying bee, without claiming a new gameplay experiment.
