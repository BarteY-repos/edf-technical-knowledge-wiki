Team IDs, radar colors and AI: community test matrix

ConversionCommunity Japanese WorkbookGameplay & AIEDF 5EDF 6
Technical reference · Translated contributed workbook notes; sample data, negative experiments and explicitly labeled inferences

Scope

The Japanese source notebook includes a test table for team IDs 0–6, with separate columns for alien/character and ant test objects, radar display, AI movement, proactive attack, counterattack and an additional column labeled 味方NPC (friendly NPC). This is a contributor test matrix, not a native engine enum or universally defined faction system.

Observed row labels

Team ID Radar entry in alien/character column Alien AI field Preemptive attack Counterattack
0 White Working Yes Yes
1 Red Working Yes Yes
2 Blue Working Yes No
3 Hidden Working No Yes
4 Hidden Working Yes Yes
5 Yellow Not working No No
6 Hidden Working No Yes

These are readings of source labels, not independently repeated in a new exact-build test. The Japanese table includes a separate 味方NPC column with non-obvious Yes/No values, for example team 1 (red, explicitly marked “enemy” elsewhere) is marked Yes. That column's operational predicate is not established from its short heading alone. Do not infer that the table marks red actors as friendly NPCs or that the game universally maps team 2 to allied actors.

The ant columns show different AI/hostility observations for several teams and have blank/dash cells; no entry should be filled in from the alien columns merely because the team ID matches. The notebook questions the status of some ant behavior (“does not move?”). Preserve those question marks.

Practical use in tools and scripts

  • Separate radar marker rendering from actual target selection, AI update enabled, attack authority, and alliance relation.
  • Never reconstruct a binary TeamManager relation matrix from a seven-row test spreadsheet alone. Match class, mode, exact build and native team callsites.
  • A white bomber marker was suggested as a possible reason for missing laser guidance in the contributor's bombardment experiment, but team ownership was not experimentally isolated there.
  • Do not assume historical EDF5/EDF6 team values share EDF4.1 native semantics.
