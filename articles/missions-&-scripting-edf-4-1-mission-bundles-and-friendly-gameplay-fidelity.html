EDF4.1 mission behavior: friendly NPCs, actors and physics

UniversalMissions & ScriptingMissionsEDF 4.1EDF 6
Source-backed research / case study · Original source branch counts, bounded geometry/sound studies, and separately classified external converted-runtime observations; live parity pending

Evidence boundary: Original EDF4.1 resources, later EDF5/EDF6 native comparisons and observed problems with converted EDF4.1 missions are distinct evidence classes. This reference describes game systems and mismatches, not a recipe for building a MissionPack or an editor. A static source match cannot certify an NPC route, playable stage or multiplayer session.

Campaign and add-on scope

The retained content-accounting comparison reports 147 distinct EDF4.1 missions across 89 base offline, 9 base online-only, 26 DLC1 and 23 DLC2 stages. A historical EDF4.1-to-EDF6 content subset included 138, omitting the nine online-only stages. The nine IDs and original online-list ordering still require direct confirmation from original MISSIONLIST.SGO; see the mission-set and BEFORE.BVM explanation.

Friendly squads and movement

Ranger, Fencer and allied actors in EDF4.1 require a coherent relationship between their scripted commands, map geometry, collision filter and navigation paths. In earlier converted EDF4.1 maps, external testing observed allies stopping at terrain/buildings, choosing paths incompatible with the source geometry, and failures of Ranger/Wing Diver recruitment. These are observations of the converted runtime, not established faults in original EDF4.1 AI. Simply transplanting donor EDF6 NAV or matching object names does not demonstrate traversal parity.

The underlying research distinguishes different questions:

  • Recruitment: whether the corresponding class has an eligible follow/recruit state, whether the interaction is exposed and whether AI follow behavior actually changes in game.
  • Movement: whether the available NAV connects start and destination regions and respects an agent's size, walkable surfaces and collision constraints.
  • Physical contact: whether the engine's rigid body/query layers allow or block particular actors against a given map feature. A Havok collision decision alone does not prove that a route exists in the NAV graph.

See original LOW/HIGH topology and source clearance and the 18-map collision cohort inventory. NPC pathfinding parity remains unverified.

Mountain maps and ground collision

The historical research tracked seven SK mountain maps with EDF5-to-EDF6 geometry-relayout differences. Its static geometry tests distinguished splitting originally flat final surfaces from changing material, winding or interior-triangle interpretation. This is a bounded cross-generation geometry observation, not proof that all seven mountains work in the EDF6 runtime or that their NAV matches EDF4.1. The earlier 16-map surface subset and later 18-map Havok body census are different populations.

Earth Eater panels and Shield Hector

An Earth Eater mission panel can have a phase-dependent lifecycle: before its encounter trigger/fly-in, a collision shell must not be assumed to behave as it does once active. The research identified Ragdoll::Disable/Ragdoll::Enable as relevant native calls in the investigated runtime. Evidence for their use in a particular compatibility implementation does not establish that every original EDF4.1 panel or every target EDF6 collision layer behaves identically. Active-panel geometry, Havok query/filter assignment and actual in-mission contact remain open.

Shield Hector research found a distinct shield rigid-body/filter path. That physical shield cannot be equated to a visual mesh or merely a projectile-damage coefficient. A proposed separate physics proxy belongs to a conversion design, not an original-game fact; the definitive original-to-target shield interaction remains runtime-pending.

DragonBig401 audio and vehicles

Static source mapping recovered five DragonBig401 sound roles, associated with shared bank tikyuu4_en_Dragon.acb. The target DragonSmall cue and cooldown representation differs; mapping filenames alone cannot establish correct roar timing, attachment, position or in-game playback. See DragonBig401 native sound roles.

Earlier modified EDF6 gameplay exhibited vehicle boarding sounds and unusual behavior in All Forces-derived vehicles. These effects may depend on third-party crew/boarding code and should not be attributed to stock EDF4.1 mechanics without independent original-game evidence. BMPT floating rear-light placement and vehicle controls are separately reported converted-runtime cases.

Mission object dependencies

One bounded EDF4.1 mission-asset source branch contained 111 mission-used object roots with 325 distinct logical dependency files:

Resource branch Files Recorded role
OBJECT/ 277 Referenced enemy, mission-object and related resources
WEAPON/EDF41_NPC/ 48 NPC equipment, model and animation resources
Other player-weapon/class packages 0 Outside the analyzed mission-dependency closure

The placement of those 48 files under WEAPON does not make them player-selectable weapons; they are pulled in by NPC object dependencies. These counts apply to the analyzed branch, not to every asset in the original game or the whole EDF6 content tree.

What remains unverified

  • Original online-only list IDs and full 147-entry direct source-list census.
  • EDF4.1 friendly NPC recruitment and pathfinding across all converted maps in EDF6 runtime.
  • Active Earth Eater panel contact/filter behavior; Shield Hector shield-body parity.
  • DragonBig401 audio playback and full vehicle behavior equivalence.
